# Style: New Chinese (新中式 / contemporary Chinese)

> Sources: Dezeen feature on Neri&Hu and Stellar Works (multi-year coverage including Stellar Works founding context); Dormakaba Blog "Modern Chinese Interior Design: 4 Shaping Trends"; AmazingArchitecture / e-architect coverage of YANLORD·TANG SONG, Wuxi (Jiangyin Bihai Courtyard) by Li Weimin, Qiu Deguang, and Wu Bin; Wallpaper* China design coverage; Stellar Works product narratives (Space Copenhagen "Ren" series inspired by Chang'e). Specific named projects cited inline.
> Last updated: 2026-04-29

## Anchor description

新中式 (New Chinese) is the late-2010s/2020s recasting of Chinese decorative tradition through a minimalist contemporary frame: it preserves the courtyard typology, the ink-painting tonal sensibility, and the joinery/silhouette language of Ming-dynasty furniture, but strips away ornate carving in favor of "simple and clean" forms employing "traditional detailing and joinery techniques" (per Dezeen / Dormakaba Blog feature on Neri&Hu's Stellar Works cabinets). The published thesis is hybridity rather than revival — "rooted in tradition but shifting toward a more modern and innovative design future" (per Dormakaba Blog "Modern Chinese Interior Design: 4 Shaping Trends"). For 3D rendering it means: an ink-painting tonal range (off-white plaster, charcoal ink, walnut/blackened wood), one or two pieces of contemporary-Ming furniture, water/stone/plant integrated as architectural element, and lighting that reads as filtered through paper or lattice.

Practical rule for an AI applying this palette: choose **one** ground (rice-paper off-white **or** ink-wash grey, not both), **one** dark wood (walnut, blackened oak, or *zitan*-tone), and **one** accent. Saturated red and saturated celadon should be used as a single 5-10% punctuation per scene (a pair of cushions, a single ceramic) — never as a wall.

## Material vocabulary

### In

- **Black walnut and traditional Chinese hardwoods (rendered as walnut for sustainability).** Stellar Works' Neri&Hu line builds its language from "wooden stacking chairs and aluminum tables that combine China's historic decorative arts with modern form and function" (per Dezeen feature on Stellar Works). The contemporary 新中式 substitutes American black walnut for the protected *zitan*/*huanghuali*, preserving silhouette while losing the heritage species.
- **Hand-troweled lime plaster, off-white or ink-wash grey.** The published 新中式 wall is plastered, not painted — the surface needs subtle tonal variation to read like ink absorbed into rice paper. (Per the broader Dormakaba Blog thesis on a "more modern touch, merging the old with the new" with traditional surface treatments retained.)
- **Bamboo — woven, not bundled.** Sozen explicitly "incorporates traditional crafting techniques into contemporary design, as seen in its bamboo-woven products" (per Dezeen / Dormakaba on Sozen). Woven shades and screens read 新中式; raw bamboo stalks read tropical / Southeast Asian.
- **Suzhou-style stone and rough-cleft limestone.** The Tang Song project explicitly uses "stone materials" alongside "planted walls" and "silk threads" to "connect the space throughout the building in harmony with the lush greenery" (per AmazingArchitecture). One placed *taihu*-style rock or cleft slab is the published convention.
- **Silk threads, raw silk, slubbed linen.** Tang Song lists "silk threads" as one of four hero materials alongside planted walls, wine cellars, and stone (per AmazingArchitecture coverage). Use silk in cushions, scroll backers, lampshade panels — never as glossy upholstery.
- **Lacquer — matte or eggshell, never high-gloss.** Traditional Chinese interiors are "characterized by lacquered surfaces" but the 新中式 move modernizes this by dropping the gloss (per Dormakaba Blog). Matte lacquered cabinet doors in ink black or 朱砂 red are the contemporary signature.
- **Translucent rice paper / *xuan zhi*, paper screens, *shoji*-equivalent panels.** Light filters rather than floods. The screen-as-wall is preserved from courtyard tradition.
- **Plants integrated as architecture — bamboo grove, pine, single specimen tree.** Tang Song's "planted walls" are not decorative pots; they are part of the architectural section (per AmazingArchitecture).

### Out

- **Heavy carved Ming/Qing reproduction with dragon/phoenix/floral relief.** Neri&Hu's deliberate move at Stellar Works was to drop "ornate carvings and etchings" in favor of "simple and clean" forms with traditional joinery (per Dezeen on Neri&Hu's dowry-cabinet line). Carved ornamental Qing-style pieces read as 古典中式 (classical Chinese), not 新中式.
- **Bright primary red as a wall color.** Traditional Chinese interiors carried "a red, gold, and black color palette" but the 新中式 thesis is the *modernization* of this — "today's designers are adding a more modern touch" (per Dormakaba Blog). Red is preserved as <10% accent, never as a 60% dominant.
- **Gold leaf at decorative scale.** Same reason: traditional gold-leaf temple decoration reads classical, not new. A small brass element is acceptable; a gilded wall is not.
- **Plastic / faux-wood "Ming-style" mass-furniture.** The Stellar Works thesis is craft authenticity — "designs include … contemporary ceramic pieces inspired by historic artifacts from the Arita Ceramic Museum" with traditional joinery (per Dezeen on Neri&Hu / Stellar Works). Mass-produced veneered "Chinese style" furniture inverts the philosophy.
- **Western Roman columns, baroque capitals, French moulding mixed in.** A defining failure mode of late-2010s "中式" developer interiors was Greco-Roman blended with Ming; the published 新中式 holds the line at Chinese-vocabulary-only.

## Lighting profile

- **Kelvin range: 2700-3000K.** Slightly cooler than wabi-sabi but warmer than commercial-modern; the ink-painting tonal range needs enough crispness to read greys against walnut, but not so much that the rice-paper wall glows blue. Cross-reference `lighting.md`; this style sits at the **mid-warm** band.
- **Filtered light through paper / lattice / screens.** The defining 新中式 lighting move is light *passing through* a material — paper lantern, *lingge* (棂格) wood lattice, woven bamboo shade. Sozen's bamboo-woven lamp shades by Jun Jie Zhang are the canonical residential fixture (per Dezeen / Dormakaba on Sozen).
- **Layered ambient + accent + reading lamp.** Ambient ceiling at 80-150 lux; per-table accents at 30-60 lux from paper-shade table lamps; one reading lamp per seated position. The published 新中式 living room is brighter than wabi-sabi but dimmer than a Western default.
- **Pendant fixtures: paper lantern, woven bamboo, brass-and-paper.** Avoid Western chandeliers and avoid bright LED panels. The pendant should read as a contemporary descendant of the *deng long* (灯笼) lantern.
- **Daylight admitted through a courtyard or shoji-equivalent screen.** Tang Song's "almost transparent patio" with one stone "half outdoors and half indoors" is the canonical daylight strategy — light enters via an interior court, not a single curtain wall (per AmazingArchitecture).
- **Cool light forbidden.** Anything ≥3500K reads developer-Chinese-mall and inverts the ink-painting palette.

## Camera bias

- **Frame the doorway / *yuan dong* (圆洞门) as foreground.** A canonical 新中式 published frame uses a circular or rectangular interior doorway as a foreground "lens" through which the viewer reads the next room — the spatial-layering thesis preserved from Suzhou garden tradition. Tang Song's "no single pillar or frame" patio is photographed by stepping back through one volume and reading through the threshold (per AmazingArchitecture).
- **Long horizontal compositions.** The Ming-furniture silhouette is horizontal and low; cameras at 1.2-1.5m eye height with a wide aspect ratio (16:9 or 2:1) read the style correctly. Vertical portrait crops are uncommon in published 新中式 interiors.
- **Asymmetric balance — one mass against void.** The Bihai Courtyard frame leaves "one stone half outdoors and half indoors" against an otherwise empty patio (per AmazingArchitecture). Compose with a single dense object (cabinet, scroll, tree) on one third and large void on the other.
- **Lens 35-50mm equivalent; vertical correction on.** Standard architectural editorial discipline.
- **Indirect / ambient daylight.** No harsh shafts, no dramatic chiaroscuro — the ink-wash sensibility wants soft even light that lets tonal greys read.

## Prop vocabulary

- **One Ming-silhouette chair or stool.** Stellar Works' Neri&Hu pieces are the contemporary canonical citation: "wooden stacking chairs … combine China's historic decorative arts with modern form and function" (per Dezeen on Stellar Works). One *guanmaoyi*-silhouette or *quanyi*-silhouette chair per room.
- **A long low cabinet / *anji* (案几) or scholar's table.** Neri&Hu's Stellar Works "line of cabinets resembling those given as dowry in Chinese culture" with simple clean form is the canonical contemporary version (per Dezeen on Neri&Hu).
- **Contemporary ceramics referencing historical forms.** Neri&Hu's "contemporary ceramic pieces inspired by historic artifacts" (per Dezeen on Stellar Works) — celadon, white porcelain with subtle crackle, or matte black. Place one large vessel, not a collection.
- **Hanging scroll (掛軸) or contemporary ink painting.** One scroll, hung high, vertical orientation. Avoid framed Western-style art.
- **Calligraphy brush / inkstone / scholar's-desk arrangement.** *Wenfang sibao* (文房四宝 — brush, ink, paper, inkstone) staged on a writing surface, restrained quantity.
- **Bonsai or single specimen plant — pine, plum, bamboo, *lan* orchid.** The "four gentlemen" plants (梅兰竹菊) are the published vocabulary; one is enough.
- **Tea set — *gongfu* tea tray with small clay teapot and cups.** Yixing-clay teapot, no glossy white teaware.
- **One placed stone — *gongshi* or *taihu*.** Tang Song's signature "one stone half outdoors and half indoors" (per AmazingArchitecture) translates at room scale to one viewing stone on a low stand.
- **Woven bamboo screen or paper-paneled folding screen.** The folding screen is the most economical "wall" in 新中式 and reads correctly even at a 60cm width.

## Reference projects

1. **Li Weimin, Qiu Deguang, and Wu Bin, *YANLORD·TANG SONG (Bihai Courtyard)*, Yushan Bay, Jiangyin (Wuxi), Jiangsu — AmazingArchitecture / e-architect coverage**. Courtyard villa development inspired by the "Ancestral Hall of the Zhu Family"; "almost transparent patio … only one stone remains half outdoors and half indoors"; planted walls, wine cellars, silk threads, and stone as the four hero materials. Qiu Deguang: "It is my ideal courtyard … it does make up for many previous regrets in my design."
2. **Neri&Hu (Lyndon Neri + Rossana Hu), *Stellar Works furniture line*, Shanghai — Dezeen / Dormakaba Blog feature**. Founded 2004; Stellar Works pieces include "wooden stacking chairs and aluminum tables" combining historic decorative arts with modern function, ceramic pieces inspired by Arita Museum artifacts, and dowry-cabinet line with simplified joinery. The defining contemporary "新中式 furniture" reference.
3. **Space Copenhagen for Stellar Works, *Ren Collection*, Shanghai — Dezeen on Stellar Works**. Furniture collection inspired by Chinese moon goddess Chang'e — Western design studio working in 新中式 vocabulary, useful as a cross-cultural boundary case showing the style can travel.
4. **David Rockwell for Stellar Works, *Ran Library bookcase* — Dezeen on Stellar Works**. Bookcase silhouette mirroring "temple doors in China" — contemporary residential cabinet that imports temple architectural language at furniture scale.
5. **Sozen (Jun Jie Zhang, founded 2011), *Bamboo-Woven Lamp Series* — Dezeen / Dormakaba Blog on Sozen**. Bamboo-woven lampshades whose "irregular shapes and curved patterns" were modeled digitally then realized via "traditional Chinese bamboo-weaving techniques." The canonical 新中式 lighting fixture reference.

## Common mistakes

1. **Carving overload — Ming/Qing reproduction read as 新中式.** Neri&Hu's explicit move was to drop "ornate carvings and etchings" in favor of "simple and clean" forms with retained joinery (per Dezeen / Dormakaba on Neri&Hu). A render with deep relief carving on cabinet doors reads 古典中式 (classical), not 新中式. Fix: simplify silhouettes; keep joinery details (mortise-and-tenon, bridle joints) but lose the dragons and floral panels.
2. **Saturated red as a wall color.** Traditional Chinese tradition carried "red, gold, and black" but the 新中式 thesis modernizes by dropping wall-scale red (per Dormakaba Blog on the modernization move). Fix: use 朱砂 red as <10% accent — one stool, one cushion pair, one lacquer panel — against a rice-paper or ink-wash dominant.
3. **Western "Asian fusion" props — Buddha statues, Thai elephants, Indian textiles.** 新中式 is *Chinese* in vocabulary; mixing Southeast Asian or South Asian motifs (Thai/Indonesian/Indian) reads as global-resort, not 新中式. Fix: hold the prop list to Chinese sources — calligraphy, *gongshi* stones, *wenfang sibao*, Yixing teaware, Suzhou silk, *gongbi* paintings.
4. **Bright cool LED panels in the ceiling.** The ink-painting palette dies under cool LED. Fix: replace with paper / bamboo / brass-and-paper pendants at 2700-3000K; treat any required architectural lighting as wall-grazing or floor-up, never as direct downlight.
5. **Plastic-veneer "Chinese style" furniture from chain retailers.** Neri&Hu and Stellar Works built their reputation on traditional joinery and craft (per Dezeen on Neri&Hu's "traditional detailing and joinery techniques"). Veneered MDF "Ming-style" furniture inverts the thesis. Fix: source solid walnut or specify joinery callouts in the model (visible mortise-and-tenon, no metal fasteners visible).
6. **Greco-Roman / Western classical mixed in (columns, pediments, baroque mouldings).** A common late-2010s developer trap; reads as Chinese-mall-luxury, not 新中式. Hold to Chinese architectural vocabulary throughout.
