# Style: Muji Minimal (極簡 Muji 風 / 無印良品)

> Sources: Kenya Hara, *Designing Design* (Lars Müller Publishers, 2007), esp. ch. 4 "White" and ch. 5 "MUJI: Nothing, Yet Everything"; Naoto Fukasawa & Jasper Morrison, *Super Normal: Sensations of the Ordinary* (Lars Müller, 2007); MAS Context interview "Intuitive Design" (Andrew Clark & Iker Gil with Naoto Fukasawa, 2014); Designboom feature on Naoto Fukasawa's Muji Wood Hut (Tokyo Design Week 2015); Dezeen 6 Nov 2015 "Muji launches trio of prefab minimal cabins by Morrison, Grcic and Fukasawa"; Dwell coverage of Muji Yō no Ie / Ki no Ie. Specific named projects cited inline.
> Last updated: 2026-04-29

## Anchor description

Muji minimal is the residential expression of Mujirushi Ryōhin's "no-brand" philosophy: a household built around ordinary objects with their intent removed (per Hara, *Designing Design*, ch. 5 "MUJI"). Kenya Hara calls the brand's controlling aesthetic *kara* — emptiness — "a vessel ready to receive any image or use" (per Hara, *Designing Design*, ch. 4 "White"). Naoto Fukasawa, Muji's design advisor since 2003, frames it as *supernormal*: design "you use immediately as normal products: to write, to eat … without thinking" (per MAS Context, "Intuitive Design," 2014). For a 3D scene this means: untreated cypress / pine / oak surfaces, paper-white walls with no paint sheen, one or two textile colors total, no logos, no metal jewelry, daylight as the hero, and every prop chosen because a Japanese household would actually use it — not because it "looks Muji."

Practical rule for an AI applying this palette: pick **paper white as dominant**, **one** softwood tone (cypress or oak — never both at once), and **one** accent that is either black or undyed flax. A second accent reads as "Scandi" or "Japandi," not Muji.

## Material vocabulary

### In

- **Untreated softwoods — cypress (hinoki), Japanese cedar (sugi), pine.** Fukasawa's Muji Wood Hut uses untreated timber inside as the room's dominant texture (per Designboom, 2015). The brand's first residential model, Ki no Ie ("Wood House," 2004, designed by Kazuhiko Nanba), establishes the same vocabulary at full residential scale (per Dwell coverage of the Muji house line).
- **Cypress plywood and oriented-strand subfloor used as finish surface.** Documented in Muji's Yō no Ie spec — "interior walls are made of untreated cypress plywood" with the construction surface treated as the final surface, not hidden (per Dwell coverage of Muji Yō no Ie).
- **Mortar / cement screed floor.** The Yō no Ie spec reads "the mortar floor finish is strong and easy to clean" — the floor is the building, not a layer added on top (per Dwell, Yō no Ie).
- **Undyed cotton, linen, and muslin.** Muji's textile aesthetic is uniformly low-saturation natural fiber; Hara's *Designing Design* treats this as the textile face of the *kara* concept — a fabric "ready to receive any image or use" (per Hara, ch. 4–5).
- **Plain stoneware and porcelain in matte glaze.** Fits the *supernormal* checklist — "objects that you use immediately as normal products" (per MAS Context interview, 2014).
- **Paper — shoji, washi lampshades, paperback books with no jacket.** White paper is, in Hara's framing, "a symbol of nonbeing … a condition which will likely be filled with content" (per Hara, ch. 4 "White").

### Out

- **Visible logos, branding, decorative typography.** Muji's brand identity is literally "no-brand quality goods" (Mujirushi Ryōhin); Hara is explicit that the products do not carry a logo (per Hara, ch. 5 "MUJI"). A scene with branded product packaging on display reads anti-Muji on first frame.
- **High-saturation accent walls, painted color blocking.** White functions as Hara's *emptiness* — adding a chartreuse feature wall converts the scene into "Scandinavian with color" (closer to Note Design Studio's *Hidden Tints*, see `scandinavian.md`) rather than Muji.
- **Polished metal — chrome, brass, bright stainless.** Fukasawa's *supernormal* objects are characterized as "things we don't find any element of design in" (per Core77 interview with Fukasawa & Morrison). Polished metal jewelry shouts design.
- **Veined marble, glossy stone.** Outside the Muji material vocabulary entirely — Muji's surfaces are matte and absorbent, not reflective.
- **Ornament, carving, pattern.** Hara: "no design" is "keeping design's intention out as much as possible" (per Hara, ch. 5 "MUJI"). Pattern always reads as intent.

## Lighting profile

- **Kelvin range: 2700-3500K** — slightly cooler than the Scandinavian baseline. Muji's residential photography across the prefab cabin series (Wood Hut, Tate no Ie, Yō no Ie) reads as soft daylight, not candle warmth. Use 2700K for evening interior fixtures and 3500K-equivalent overcast daylight for the architectural reads (per Dezeen feature on the Muji prefab cabin trio, 6 Nov 2015, and Designboom Wood Hut images).
- **Daylight is the primary light source.** The Wood Hut "large sliding glass panels" are the published architectural move — the interior is lit by an entire wall of glazing, not by a hero ceiling pendant (per Designboom 2015). Render with a large soft skylight key, not a tungsten point-light.
- **Fixtures — paper, frosted glass, simple cylinders.** Muji's own lighting catalog vernacular is paper drum shades, white globes, and small bedside reading lamps. The fixtures should disappear into the architecture rather than function as sculpture (consistent with Hara's "the suitable thing prepared for people without them noticing it," per Hara, ch. 5).
- **No candles by default.** Unlike Scandinavian (where candles are cultural — see `scandinavian.md`), Muji's published interiors do not use candles as a stylistic anchor. The light is daylight + functional task lamp, full stop.
- **Layer mix — ambient at 80-150 lx, task at 200-400 lx, no decorative third layer.** Two-layer lighting is correct for Muji; adding the "many small caves of light" Scandinavian third layer pushes the scene toward hygge, which is a different style.

## Camera bias

- **Wide, soft, low-contrast daylight.** The recurring framing convention in the Muji prefab cabin press set is a 28-35mm equivalent looking through one wall of glazing into a corner where wall + floor + sliding door stack as flat planes (per Dezeen 2015 image set on the Muji cabin trio).
- **Eye height 1.1-1.4m** — the Japanese residential floor-living convention, lower than the Western 1.4-1.6m sofa-height convention. The Wood Hut's documented interior shots sit close to floor cushion height (per Designboom Wood Hut image set, 2015).
- **Vertical correction on, but framing tolerates more negative space than Western publications.** Hara's *kara* / emptiness applies to the photograph itself — the published Muji interiors leave large blank wall and ceiling areas as the subject, not as background (per Hara, ch. 4 "White").
- **Foreground prop optional, background plane mandatory.** A Muji shot can succeed with no foreground subject as long as the background plane (a paper-white wall, a single window, a strip of cypress floor) is clean and held in deep focus.

## Prop vocabulary

Build the prop list directly from Fukasawa's *supernormal* selection criterion — objects "everyone recognizes and perceives as normal" (per Core77 / Fukasawa & Morrison, *Super Normal*, 2007):

- **Plain wooden chair — Wegner-Y / Wishbone, or Muji's own oak dining chair.** The chair as a *supernormal* category is Fukasawa's own touchstone — see his Maruni Hiroshima Arm Chair (often called the "Muji-friendly" Wegner heir).
- **Single futon or platform bed — undressed, no headboard, white or oat linen.** The Yō no Ie published rooms render the bed as platform + linen, no upholstered head (per Dwell, Yō no Ie).
- **Books — paperback spines, no decorative jackets.** Hara: paper is the carrier of *kara* (per Hara, ch. 4). A bookshelf of jacketless paperbacks reads more Muji than coffee-table monographs do.
- **One houseplant — fiddle leaf fig, Japanese maple bonsai, or potted bamboo.** Limit to one; multiplicity reads as "wellness boutique," not Muji.
- **Single ceramic kettle / teapot in matte black or unglazed clay.** The Wood Hut interior places one black object against the cypress field as the scene's punctuation (per Designboom, 2015).
- **Plain woven basket (rattan / sea grass).** Low laundry basket or shoebox-format storage on a shelf — Muji's own catalog uses these as the universal storage prop.
- **Folded cotton blanket, neatly stacked — never thrown / draped.** Critical contrast with Scandinavian, where blankets must look "lived in" (see `scandinavian.md`). Muji rooms read as *prepared*; a draped throw breaks the read.

## Reference projects

1. **Naoto Fukasawa, *Muji Wood Hut*, Tokyo Design Week 2015 — Designboom, 2 Nov 2015** ("naoto fukasawa embodies his idea of peacefulness in a timber retreat muji hut") and Dezeen, 6 Nov 2015 ("Muji launches trio of prefab minimal cabins by Morrison, Grcic and Fukasawa"). Black-tarred timber exterior, exposed cypress interior, pitched roof, single sliding glass wall.
2. **Konstantin Grcic, *Muji Aluminium Hut*, Tokyo Design Week 2015 — Dezeen / ArchDaily 2015** ("Muji Unveils Tiny, Pre-Fabricated 'Huts'"). Useful counter-example: shows the supernormal approach applied to industrial aluminium rather than wood — same emptiness, different surface.
3. **Jasper Morrison, *Muji Cork Hut*, Tokyo Design Week 2015 — Dezeen, 6 Nov 2015**. Cork-clad cabin; rounds out the canonical trio that defines Muji's published residential vocabulary.
4. **Kazuhiko Nanba for Muji, *Ki no Ie* ("Wood House"), 2004, ongoing — Muji Japan housing catalogue; covered in Dwell housing line retrospective**. The 20-year baseline residential model that grounds the Muji residential aesthetic before the Hut series.
5. **Muji, *Yō no Ie* and *Tate no Ie* (Vertical House), 2014 onward — Architizer, "How Muji Smashed the Preconceived Limits of Prefabricated Architecture"; Dwell, "Muji Just Unveiled a New Prefab Home"**. Open plan, no interior walls, untreated cypress plywood, mortar floor — the canonical Muji urban-house.
6. **Naoto Fukasawa & Jasper Morrison, *Super Normal* exhibition + book (Lars Müller, 2007)** — fixture/object reference; the 204-object selection is the closest thing to a Muji prop bible and includes the canonical Swiss Rex peeler, Maruni chair, and the household objects Muji and *supernormal* both endorse.

## Common mistakes

1. **"Muji = white IKEA."** Defaulting to bare white walls + birch plywood + IKEA Poäng does not produce Muji. Fukasawa's distinguishing move is *untreated* surface — cypress that smells, mortar that shows tool marks, cotton that wrinkles (per Designboom Wood Hut, 2015; Dwell Yō no Ie). Sealed, sanded-to-perfection finishes read mass-market Scandi. Fix: leave at least one wood and one floor surface unlacquered; let knots and grain show.
2. **Treating Muji as Japandi.** Japandi adds Scandinavian warmth (sheepskin, candles, a sage accent) on top of Japanese minimalism. Hara's MUJI is *not* Japandi — it is the bare emptiness side of the equation (per Hara, ch. 5 "MUJI"). Fix: if rendering true Muji, omit the wool throw, the sheepskin, and the moody accent wall — those belong in `scandinavian.md`.
3. **Decorative ornament — abstract art, decorative pillows, vases of cut flowers.** Hara: "no design" means "keeping design's intention out as much as possible" (per Hara, ch. 5). A decorative vase with eucalyptus on the dining table reads as Pinterest-Japandi, not Muji. Fix: replace the cut-flower vase with a single potted plant or empty ceramic.
4. **Logo product / branded packaging visible.** Muji's identity is literally "Mujirushi" — *no logo* (per Hara, ch. 5). A render with branded coffee packaging, a labeled olive oil bottle, or a logo'd appliance breaks the read instantly. Fix: model decanted goods (clear glass jars with handwritten kraft labels, or unlabeled ceramic).
5. **Throwing the blanket.** Muji rooms are *prepared*, not *lived-in*. The draped-blanket Scandinavian convention is wrong here. Fix: fold the blanket; align it with a shelf edge; treat textiles as architecture, not as casual layer.
