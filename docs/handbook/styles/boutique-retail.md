# Style: Boutique Retail (精品零售小店)

> Sources: Wallpaper* "Aesop stores: a visual history of interior architecture"; Dezeen "Dezeen's top 10 Aesop store designs," 17 December 2014; Designboom 8 January 2019 on Aesop Shibuya by Torafu Architects; Designboom 7 July 2020 on Aesop Yokohama (indigo + maple) by Torafu Architects + buaisou; Designboom 2 November 2016 on Aesop Samcheong, Seoul by Torafu Architects; ArchDaily on Aesop Grand Front Osaka by Torafu Architects; Dezeen 25 May 2013 on Aesop Shibuya (earlier 2013 store) by Torafu Architects; Snøhetta project pages and Wallpaper* coverage of Aesop Sloane Square (London) and Aesop Sydney; Sophie Hicks Architects' page on the Acne Studios Seoul flagship; Dezeen 23 September 2015, "Sophie Hicks conceals 'concrete monster' within translucent walls for Acne Studios' Seoul flagship"; Dezeen 9 August 2022 on Acne Studios Rue Saint Honoré, Paris by Arquitectura-G; Highsnobiety 2024 on Gentle Monster Haus Nowhere Seoul; Grazia 2024 on Haus Nowhere; Gentle Monster official "Haus Dosan" page; Insider Trends "Inside the magical retail design world of Gentle Monster."
> Last updated: 2026-04-29

## Anchor description

The contemporary boutique-retail genre — as exemplified by Aesop, Acne Studios, Le Labo, Byredo, COS, and Gentle Monster — is a small-format hospitality-grade retail interior in which the architectural envelope and material specificity carry as much brand weight as the product. Each store is treated as a site-specific architectural commission rather than a rolled-out catalogue fitout: "Established in Melbourne in 1987, Aesop has become one of the most respected beauty brands in the world … Aesop has developed strong relationships with local architects and designers in the creation of its stores. From London to Singapore, New York to Kyoto, Aesop's approach to retail design and architecture is unique and respectful of community, culture and history" (per Wallpaper*, *Aesop stores: a visual history of interior architecture*). The defining contemporary references include Snøhetta's Aesop Sloane Square (a Devonshire-clay-plastered "architectural tree" with a 4.5 m² water table — per Wallpaper*), Sophie Hicks' Acne Studios Seoul flagship (a translucent lightbox concealing a "concrete monster" — per Dezeen 23 September 2015), and Torafu Architects' Aesop Yokohama (indigo-dyed maple boards forming "a gentle curve like a ship's hull" — per Designboom 7 July 2020). For a 3D scene this means: a single dominant material per store (one stone, one wood, one concrete), restrained palette, generous floor-to-product ratio, hospitality-grade demonstration sink at the entrance, and a single hero light element.

Practical rule for an AI applying this palette: pick **one** dominant volume material (concrete, plaster, gypsum, or stone), **one** secondary timber-or-textile move (indigo-dyed maple, cross-grain cedar, blackened steel), and **one** brand-color accent (Aesop umber, Acne acid-yellow, Gentle Monster industrial gray). The genre's discipline is *one strong site-specific gesture*, not a layered look.

## Material vocabulary

### In

- **Raw concrete with timber-formwork imprint.** Sophie Hicks' Acne Studios Seoul: "Structurally, it is a rectangular building on two floors, both of rough concrete, with four pairs of concrete columns holding up the roof and the upper floor … The concrete bears the imprint of rough timber such as that used to make boardwalks to the beach in the Stockholm archipelago" (per Dezeen 23 September 2015). The same vocabulary recurs at Acne Studios Paris Rue Saint Honoré, where Arquitectura-G drew on "a skate park situated beneath a concrete bridge in Stockholm" (per Dezeen 9 August 2022).
- **Devonshire clay plaster, lime plaster, gypsum.** Snøhetta's signature texture for Aesop — Sloane Square's "Devonshire clay-plaster clad column" arching across the ceiling "like an architectural tree" (per Wallpaper* on Aesop Sloane Square).
- **Glass-fiber reinforced concrete (GRC).** Aesop Oslo by Snøhetta uses GRC for "interior fixtures and displays" — co-created with Aesop's Paris-based design team (per Wallpaper*).
- **Stone — sandstone, granite, fieldstone — at threshold and counter.** Aesop Sydney's "robust material palette of stone, brass metal and concrete, to slow down the pace of passersby and encourage browsing" — the granite-to-sandstone transition is a deliberate slowing device (per Wallpaper*). Snøhetta's strategy: roughness slows the body.
- **Indigo-dyed maple, cross-grain cedar, chestnut, reclaimed timber.** Torafu's Yokohama uses maple stained by indigo dyers buaisou into a "gentle curve like a ship's hull"; Samcheong uses Japanese cedar with cross-grain face exposed; the 2013 Shibuya store uses "reclaimed timber flooring" at the entrance with sisal carpet beyond (per Designboom 7 July 2020; Designboom 2 November 2016; Dezeen 25 May 2013).
- **Blackened steel, galvanized steel, brass.** Recurring across Aesop Shibuya 2013 ("blackened steel shelves," "elongated demonstration sink … made from blackened steel, with a mirrored splashback from which simple garden taps protrude" — per Dezeen 25 May 2013), Shibuya 2019 ("galvanised steel cladding"), and Aesop Sydney ("brass metal" — per Wallpaper*).
- **Mirrored back wall — antique-style.** Aesop Oslo: "An antique-style, though modern-make, mirror lines the back wall, creating the effect of a much larger retail space; it also conceals a small office and storage" (per Wallpaper*).
- **Translucent polycarbonate / fiber-glass exterior wrapper.** Acne Studios Seoul's exterior is a "translucent and ghostly but, in essence, plain and simple" lightbox concealing the concrete interior (per Dezeen 23 September 2015).
- **Demonstration sink at or near the entrance.** Hospitality-grade detail unique to Aesop's vocabulary: the 2013 Shibuya store features "an elongated demonstration sink … just inside the entrance" (per Dezeen 25 May 2013); the Sloane Square store has "four tin bath-sized fibre glass sinks, used for skin consultations" (per Wallpaper*).
- **Squid-fishing-boat pendant lights (project-specific).** Torafu's Aesop Kyoto: "By utilising the height of the vaulted ceiling, we hung squid fishing boat pendant lights like a mobile and filled the space with light" — example of the genre's commitment to site-and-craft specificity (per ArchDaily / Designboom on Aesop Kyoto).

### Out

- **Mass-market retail fixtures — slatwall, gondola shelving, chrome racks.** The genre is defined by bespoke fixtures; Sophie Hicks' Seoul fixtures are softly reflective metal walls "freestanding in the open space" (per Dezeen 23 September 2015), and Snøhetta's Oslo fixtures are GRC.
- **High-density product display.** Aesop and Acne stores are deliberately under-merchandised — the floor-to-product ratio is high, with single rows of products rather than walls of stock.
- **Glowing backlit signage, oversized logo on facade.** The genre relies on architectural recognition, not signage. Acne Studios Seoul's facade is a translucent box, not a logo wall (per Dezeen 23 September 2015).
- **Uniform global rollout aesthetic.** "Christian Halleröd … the premises see an understated interior design by Swedish architect Christian Halleröd" — but each project still has a site-specific gesture (e.g. Shanghai's red display tables and dark stone facade — per Retail Design Blog 2020). A literal copy-paste fitout breaks the genre.
- **Cool-white retail light at >4000K.** Most Aesop interiors are warm-neutral (3000–3500K) to flatter the brown-glass bottles; Acne Studios is more daylight-neutral but still controlled. Big-box retail cool-white reads anti-genre.
- **Themed interiors with overt branding.** Even Gentle Monster's most maximalist Haus Nowhere is branded by atmosphere — "the stores even have their own unique fragrance and soundtrack to ensure that none are totally alike" (per Insider Trends *Inside the magical retail design world of Gentle Monster*) — not by logo signage.

## Lighting profile

- **Kelvin range: 3000–3500K.** Warm-neutral; warm enough to flatter timber and brown glass, cool enough to read as retail not residential.
- **Layer mix: ambient even + product accent + threshold drama.** Translate to ambient at 200–350 lx, product-shelf accent at 500–800 lx, threshold/feature at 100–200 lx (the entrance is typically darker than the merch zone, drawing the body inward).
- **Single hero gesture per store.** Sloane Square's Devonshire-clay column has water cascading into four sinks; the column itself reads as the lighting architecture (per Wallpaper*). Aesop Kyoto's hanging "squid-fishing-boat pendant lights like a mobile" form the room's lighting sculpture (per Torafu Architects on Aesop Kyoto).
- **Translucent envelope as ambient diffuser.** Acne Studios Seoul: "translucent: viewed from the outside, it is a restrained, rather elegant lightbox" — the entire exterior wrapper diffuses daylight into the concrete interior (per Dezeen 23 September 2015).
- **Backlit display niches.** Common across Aesop, Acne, and Le Labo — small recessed shelves with linear LED at the shelf face.
- **Demonstration sink as wet-light feature.** The Aesop sink is lit as a hospitality fixture, not a utility — small pendant or sconce above the basin.

## Camera bias

- **35–50mm equivalent, eye-height standing (1.5–1.7 m).** Where cafes shoot from seated, retail shoots from standing-customer height — preserves the feeling of approaching the merch wall.
- **One-product hero shot.** Boutique-retail publication photography (Wallpaper*, Dezeen, Designboom, ArchDaily) consistently uses a single product (one Aesop bottle, one Acne shoe) on a long shelf as the foreground hero with the architectural envelope behind.
- **Threshold/entrance shot from outside in.** Acne Studios Seoul's published photography frames the translucent box from across the street, then the concrete interior from the entry threshold (per Dezeen 23 September 2015). Aesop Yokohama is photographed from the street into the curving indigo wall (per Designboom 7 July 2020).
- **Low-angle architectural detail.** The Sloane Square clay-plaster column or the Aesop Kyoto squid-pendant cluster is shot from below to emphasize the architectural-tree / mobile reading (per Wallpaper*; per Torafu / Aesop Kyoto coverage).
- **Vertical correction on. Centerline-symmetry where the architecture is symmetric.** Sophie Hicks' Acne Seoul layout (rectangular footprint, four pairs of concrete columns, central floating staircase) is shot symmetrically (per Dezeen 23 September 2015).

## Prop vocabulary

- **Brand product, single row, generously spaced.** One row of Aesop amber-brown bottles on a long shelf; one row of Acne shoes on a steel rack; restraint over density.
- **Demonstration sink, with simple garden taps.** Aesop signature; "an elongated demonstration sink … with a mirrored splashback from which simple garden taps protrude" (per Dezeen 25 May 2013 on Aesop Shibuya).
- **Bespoke furniture by named designer.** Acne Studios uses Max Lamb across multiple stores; Aesop commissions site-specific fixtures from each project's lead architect (per Wallpaper* on Aesop's design relationships; Retail Design Blog 2020 on Christian Halleröd / Max Lamb collaboration).
- **One textile note — sisal carpet, wool runner, linen cushion.** Aesop Shibuya 2013's "remainder of the space is finished with sisal carpet" after a reclaimed-timber entry strip (per Dezeen 25 May 2013).
- **Single bouquet of dried herbs / cut branch / single plant.** No flowering arrangements — the genre prefers dried, neutral, sculptural greenery.
- **Brown apothecary glass bottle as product *and* prop.** Aesop's bottle is part of the architectural vocabulary — "Rectangles of brown glass surround the doorway, referencing the trademark brown bottles that line the walls of the store" (per Dezeen 25 May 2013).
- **Antique mirror, full-wall.** Aesop Oslo's antique-style mirror "lines the back wall, creating the effect of a much larger retail space" (per Wallpaper*).
- **Site-specific local craft prop.** Yokohama's indigo-dyed timber by buaisou (per Designboom 7 July 2020); Kyoto's squid-fishing-boat pendants (per Torafu / Aesop Kyoto).

## Reference projects

1. **Aesop Sloane Square, London — Snøhetta, opened 2017.** Aesop's 200th global store; ~100 m²; Devonshire clay-plaster column "spanning the ceiling like 'an architectural tree'"; 4.5 m² central water table with "slim veil of water cascading into four tin bath-sized fibre glass sinks, used for skin consultations" (per Wallpaper* feature on Aesop Sloane Square; senior Snøhetta architect Peter Girgis quoted).
2. **Aesop Oslo — Snøhetta.** Gypsum-clad brick walls; GRC interior fixtures; antique-style mirror back wall concealing office and storage; co-created with Aesop's Paris-based design team (per Wallpaper*).
3. **Aesop Sydney — Snøhetta.** Aesop's largest Snøhetta store; "robust material palette of stone, brass metal and concrete, to slow down the pace of passersby and encourage browsing"; granite-to-sandstone transition at entry as deliberate-slowing device (per Wallpaper*).
4. **Aesop Shibuya, Tokyo — Torafu Architects, opened 2019.** Tenth retail outlet for Aesop by Torafu; 7 m frontage × 12 m depth; galvanised-steel cladding for facade and metal fittings; arch-shaped eaves echoing the next-door building (per Designboom 8 January 2019).
5. **Aesop Yokohama — Torafu Architects + buaisou collective, opened 2020.** L-shaped 7.4 m × 16 m site within Newoman mall; indigo-dyed maple boards (ai-some) forming "a gentle curve like a ship's hull over the rough mortar floor" (per Designboom 7 July 2020).
6. **Aesop Samcheong, Seoul — Torafu Architects, opened 2016.** Ninth Aesop store for Torafu; near Gyeongbokgung Palace; 5 m × 7.5 m existing single-storey building; original wooden truss exposed; midnight-blue exterior + warm off-white interior (per Designboom 2 November 2016).
7. **Aesop Shibuya 2013 — Torafu Architects, opened 2013.** Long, narrow shop; mirrored wall + protruding blackened-steel demonstration sink; brown-glass rectangles framing the doorway echoing Aesop's amber bottles; reclaimed-timber entrance with sisal carpet beyond (per Dezeen 25 May 2013).
8. **Aesop Kyoto — Torafu Architects.** Repurposed metal pipes and lightbulbs from squid-fishing boats hung "like a mobile" beneath a vaulted ceiling (per Torafu Architects / Aesop Kyoto coverage).
9. **Acne Studios Seoul flagship — Sophie Hicks Architects, opened 2015.** Gangnam district; 230 m² over two floors; translucent lightbox exterior; rough concrete interior with timber-formwork imprint; four pairs of concrete columns; floating concrete staircase wrapping a column; softly reflective metal display walls (per Dezeen 23 September 2015 / Sophie Hicks Architects project page).
10. **Acne Studios Rue Saint Honoré, Paris — Arquitectura-G, opened 2022.** "Monolithic" store referencing a Stockholm skatepark and its Parisian setting; founder/CCO Jonny Johansson cited a Stockholm concrete-bridge skate park as inspiration (per Dezeen 9 August 2022).
11. **Acne Studios Berlin — Bubab Architects (Michail Blösser).** ~260 m²; steel partitions, elongated tables, considered lighting "resemble curatorial gestures as much as commercial design" (per Bubab Architects / Thisispaper).
12. **Gentle Monster Haus Nowhere Seoul — opened 5 September 2024.** 13-story headquarters and flagship in Seongsu; first 5 floors public; immersive multi-floor "future retail" treatment with animatronic dachshund installation; rotating exhibitions including Max Siedentopf's *More Is More* (per Highsnobiety 2024; Grazia 2024 — *Multi-Sense Retail*).
13. **Gentle Monster Haus Dosan, Seoul.** "Beginning of future retail, the HAUS concept"; combines Gentle Monster, Tamburins (cosmetics), and Nudake (dessert) into one experiential space; "TRUTH OR DARE" mixed-media installation by Jonas Lindstroem; THE PROBE six-legged robot by Gentle Monster's Robotics Lab (per Gentle Monster official Haus Dosan project page).
14. **Gentle Monster Bukchon, Seoul.** Adaptive reuse of a 46-year-old bathhouse; original brick stairs preserved against a contemporary interior (per Insider Trends; Creatrip).

## Common mistakes

1. **Treating the store as catalogue rollout — copy-paste fitout.** The genre's defining commitment is site-specificity. Wallpaper* explicitly: Aesop "has developed strong relationships with local architects and designers in the creation of its stores. From London to Singapore, New York to Kyoto, Aesop's approach to retail design and architecture is unique and respectful of community, culture and history" (per Wallpaper*). Fix: pick one local material or technique (indigo dye, squid-pendant lights, Devonshire clay) and let it carry the room.
2. **Density over restraint.** Boutique-retail interiors are deliberately under-merchandised. Sophie Hicks' Acne Seoul holds clothing on "softly reflective metal walls, freestanding in the open space" with no decoration (per Dezeen 23 September 2015). Cramming product into every shelf reads as fast-retail, not boutique.
3. **Heavy logo / signage substituting for architecture.** Acne Studios Seoul's facade is *translucent*, not branded; Aesop's facades are typically unbranded except for a small etched-glass mark. The genre's recognition comes from architecture, not signage.
4. **Cool-white LED at >4000K.** Aesop's brown-glass bottles and Acne's concrete read warm-neutral; cool-white kills both. Fix: clamp 3000–3500K; never go above 4000K for boutique-retail scenes.
5. **Skipping the threshold/transition.** Snøhetta's Aesop Sydney uses "the roughness of the granite entrance in contrast to the smooth sandstone features of the shop interior" specifically to create "a moment of stillness upon entry" (per Wallpaper*). Acne Studios Seoul's translucent-to-concrete transition is the entire architectural premise (per Dezeen 23 September 2015). A flat continuous floor with no threshold material change loses the genre's slowing device.
6. **Mistaking Gentle Monster's maximalism as a license for chaos.** Even Haus Nowhere is choreographed — "every element — from architecture and interiors to music, campaigns, and even food — is aligned to tell one story" (per Grazia 2024). The maximalism is curatorial, not random. Fix: if the brief is Gentle-Monster-mode, pick one narrative (e.g. "More Is More," "TIME-SLEEP," "UNOPENED: FUTURE") and theme every element to that story; do not throw unrelated installations into one room.
