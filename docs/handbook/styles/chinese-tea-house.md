# Style: Chinese Tea House (国风茶馆 / Contemporary Guofeng Tea House)

> Sources: Designboom 16 June 2021 on the Fuzhou Teahouse / The Relic Shelter by Neri&Hu; Dezeen 2 October 2022, "Neri&Hu incorporates historic wooden structure into copper-clad tea rooms"; ArchDaily, *Fuzhou Teahouse / Neri&Hu Design and Research Office* (2021); Surface magazine 2021 feature on Neri&Hu's Fuzhou teahouse; Hospitality Design feature on the Fuzhou Teahouse; Neri&Hu *The Relic Shelter | Fuzhou Tea House* official project page; ArchDaily 2014 on ChenJiaShan Park Tea Lounge by Atelier Liu Yuyang Architects (ALYA); Dezeen 14 October 2019 on Atelier Liu Yuyang's XY Yunlu Hotel/Yun House (Yangshuo); Dezeen feature on Tingtai Teahouse, Shanghai by Linehouse; e-architect / dezeen feature on Bamboo Courtyard Teahouse, Yangzhou by Sun Wei / HWCD; Designboom 29 December 2024 on the Tea House by Qicang Design; Dezeen "tea-houses" tag.
> Last updated: 2026-04-29

## Anchor description

The contemporary Chinese tea-house (中式茶馆 / 国风茶馆) is the renewed architectural and interior-design genre that re-interprets Ming and Qing tea-house vocabulary — the courtyard (院), the lattice screen (花窗), the rough rammed-earth or grey-brick wall (灰砖), the elevated wooden hall (堂) — in contemporary materials and tectonics. The defining contemporary canon is Neri&Hu's *The Relic Shelter | Fuzhou Tea House* (2021), which "envisioned [the building] as a house atop a rock," wrapping a relocated Qing-dynasty Hui-style timber residence in "rammed concrete tinted with a terracotta hue and sweeping copper-clad trusses that follow the roofline of the wooden structure" (per Designboom 16 June 2021; Dezeen 2 October 2022; ArchDaily). For a 3D scene this means: warm grey or terracotta-tinted rammed-concrete walls, dark elm or pine timber columns and ceiling beams, latticed window screens, paper-shade or hand-blown-glass low pendants, a courtyard or sky-well bringing filtered daylight inward, and tea-set vignettes on dark wood tables under warm 2400–2700K light.

Practical rule for an AI applying this palette: pick **one** dominant heavy-mass surface (rammed concrete OR grey brick OR rammed earth), **one** timber lineage (dark elm/pine for traditional, recycled ash for contemporary), and **one** metal accent (copper for the Neri&Hu mode, brass for the ALYA mode). Do not stack vermillion lacquer + carved dragons + brocade upholstery — that vocabulary belongs to imperial-palace pastiche, not contemporary Guofeng.

## Material vocabulary

### In

- **Rammed concrete or rammed earth, terracotta-tinted.** Neri&Hu's Fuzhou move: rammed concrete "tinted with a terracotta hue" wraps the historic structure (per Dezeen 2 October 2022). The wall is heavy, monolithic, and warmly toned — the opposite of polished plaster. Lyndon Neri: "we decided to wrap the structure with a massive wall of rammed concrete. It creates a mezzanine that guests may climb onto and observe the structure from the outside looking back in" (per Surface 2021).
- **Chinese grey brick (青砖) and reclaimed brick.** The Bamboo Courtyard Teahouse uses brick as both heat-retention mass and visual order (per e-architect on Tea House Yangzhou). Brick should read aged or hand-laid, mortar visible.
- **Dark elm, pine, or zelkova timber for columns, beams, and ceiling.** The Hui-style Qing-dynasty timber residence at the heart of Fuzhou Teahouse is "replete with ornamental carvings and intricate joinery" (per Designboom 16 June 2021). When historic timber is unavailable, recycled Chinese ash is the contemporary substitute (per ArchDaily on ALYA's Wuyuan Rd. Studio).
- **Copper trusses, copper-clad detailing, brass dividers.** Fuzhou Teahouse uses "sweeping copper-clad trusses" as the contemporary contrast against the historic wooden hall (per Dezeen 2 October 2022); ALYA's Wuyuan Rd. Studio uses brass dividers and copper hand pull-rods (per ArchDaily).
- **River-pebble terrazzo flooring.** ALYA's "gray-colored river pebble terrazzo floor goes well with brass divider and margin" (per ArchDaily Wuyuan Rd. Studio) — pebble-aggregate is a Chinese contemporary signature, distinct from the European terrazzo tradition.
- **Bamboo — live (potted), split (slat), or whole (structural culm).** Bamboo Courtyard Teahouse uses bamboo structurally; XinHeYe Tea House by Qicang Design uses a "black timber facade that integrates with the verdant surroundings" (per Designboom 29 December 2024).
- **Lattice screens (花窗).** Geometric wooden window screens — variants include 步步锦 (bù-bù-jǐn, "step-step brocade"), 万字纹 (swastika repeat), and 冰裂纹 (ice-crack pattern). Used as window infill, room divider, and ceiling baffle.
- **Sky-well (天井) and courtyard.** Neri&Hu's Fuzhou Teahouse: "sky wells penetrate the roof, bringing natural light into the depths of the enclosure and illuminating the priceless artefact on display" (per Surface 2021). The courtyard or sky-well is structurally and ritually central to the genre.
- **Paper or hand-blown glass low pendants, single fixture per zone.** Warm low-Kelvin only.

### Out

- **Imperial-yellow ceiling, cinnabar columns at full-strength, gold-leaf wall panels.** That vocabulary is Forbidden City pastiche — not the contemporary Guofeng tea-house genre that Neri&Hu, ALYA, Linehouse, and Atelier Deshaus have built (per Surface 2021 on Neri&Hu's stated avoidance of overt nationalism in favor of "a connection between old and new"; Dezeen on Atelier Deshaus' tiny tea pavilion's spare modern glass-walled treatment).
- **Carved-dragon furniture, gilded screens, brocaded cushions in primary roles.** Same reason — reads as theme restaurant, not architectural tea-house. Where ornament appears, it is in the *preserved* historic timber (Fuzhou Teahouse's Hui-style residence carvings) seen *against* a stripped-down contemporary shell, not on new-fabricated furniture.
- **High-gloss white quartz, mirror chrome, glass-and-steel showroom finishes.** The genre is heavy, tactile, age-tolerant. Surface explicitly: "rammed concrete tinted with a terracotta hue" + "rough, earth-coloured concrete, plaster and copper" finishes the secondary spaces (per Dezeen 2 October 2022; Surface 2021 on Fuzhou).
- **Cool-white LED light.** Same as the Japanese genre — kills the texture of brick, rammed earth, and timber grain.
- **Background music: instrumental erhu cliché.** The genre's reference projects rely on architectural quiet, sky-well ambient sound, and tea-pouring rhythm — not pre-recorded "Chinese restaurant" instrumentation. (Cross-reference: Atelier Deshaus tea house in Shanghai West Bund — a 130 sq ft glass-walled pavilion organized around acoustic stillness.)

## Lighting profile

- **Kelvin range: 2400–2700K.** Warm, similar to the Japanese tea-cafe — required to read brick, rammed concrete, and dark timber without flattening the surfaces.
- **Layer mix: sky-well daylight + low ambient + table lamps.** Translate to ambient at 80–150 lx, table-task at 200–300 lx (sufficient to read tea-leaf color in the cup), decorative pendants at 30–60 lx.
- **Sky-well / oculus is the hero light.** Neri&Hu Fuzhou: "Sky wells penetrate the roof, bringing natural light into the depths of the enclosure and illuminating the priceless artefact on display" (per Surface 2021). Also Neri&Hu's "carved oculus capped by glass is submerged beneath the pool in the courtyard above. It filters the sun through a thin film of water, creating a mesmerizing play of reflections" (per Surface 2021). Render with strong directional sun through a roof aperture, falling onto a single timber column or a single tea-table.
- **Lattice-window light-filter.** The 花窗 lattice broken into geometric shadows on a brick or rammed-concrete wall is the genre's signature lighting effect.
- **Single low pendant per tea-table.** Hand-blown glass, paper, or copper shade; suspended ~80 cm above the table; warm tungsten-mimic at 2400K.
- **Candle / oil-lamp / hand-held lantern as decorative accent.** Used in vignette photography of the genre (e.g. ChenJiaShan Park Tea Lounge published photography by Jeremy San per ArchDaily 2014).

## Camera bias

- **35–50mm equivalent, eye-height seated (1.0–1.3 m).** Tea-house genre publication framing is from the seated guest's POV at the tea-table — preserves the feeling of being held by the room.
- **Sky-well / courtyard as compositional center.** Neri&Hu's published photography of Fuzhou consistently centers the sky-well's beam of light falling on the historic timber structure (per Surface 2021; Designboom 16 June 2021).
- **Mezzanine view "looking back in."** Lyndon Neri's stated framing: the rammed-concrete mezzanine "creates a mezzanine that guests may climb onto and observe the structure from the outside looking back in" (per Surface 2021). The genre's hero shot is *through* lattice or *across* mezzanine onto the timber hall.
- **Foreground prop: tea-set vignette — gaiwan, fairness cup, tea-tray.** One staged tea service in the foreground, mid-pour or just-poured.
- **Vertical correction on. Use ceiling beam lines as compositional anchor.** Dark timber beams running into the depth of the frame are the genre's leading lines.
- **Close-up macro of joinery or carving.** Where the historic timber is preserved (Fuzhou Teahouse's Qing residence "ornamental carvings and intricate joinery" per Designboom 16 June 2021), the carving detail is published as a standalone image.

## Prop vocabulary

- **Gaiwan (盖碗) tea-set — lidded bowl, pitcher (公道杯), small cups (品茗杯), tea tray (茶盘).** The functional core of the genre; each tea-table is staged with a complete set.
- **Yixing zisha (宜兴紫砂) clay teapot.** Unglazed purple-clay teapot is the connoisseur's vessel; matte texture, hand-thrown silhouette.
- **Tea-tasting tray — bamboo, slate, or river-pebble inlay.** ALYA's "river pebble terrazzo" detail informs the prop language (per ArchDaily Wuyuan Rd.).
- **Single calligraphy scroll — vertical hanging, neutral-toned ink on rice paper.** One scroll per primary wall, never multiple. Restraint is the genre's discipline.
- **Lattice-window screen (花窗).** As both architectural element and movable prop divider.
- **Single bonsai or single-stem ikebana-style branch.** One specimen plant; potted plum or tall corkscrew willow on a low timber side-table.
- **Tea storage jar — glazed ceramic with wood lid.** Lined up on an open shelf; 2–4 jars maximum.
- **Stone water-basin or carved stone trough at the threshold.** Used in courtyard/sky-well staging.
- **Linen cushion, single low cushion per chair, neutral cream / sand / charcoal.** Restrained textile palette.

## Reference projects

1. **The Relic Shelter | Fuzhou Tea House, Fuzhou, Gulou District — Neri&Hu Design and Research Office (Lyndon Neri & Rossana Hu), completed 2021.** ~20,000 sq ft / ~1,860 m². Encloses a relocated Qing-dynasty Hui-style timber residence within rammed concrete tinted terracotta + copper-clad trusses + sky-wells + a sub-grade rotunda with an oculus submerged below a pool in the courtyard above. Inspired by Scottish photographer John Thomson's 1871 image of Jinshan Temple on the Min River. Won Grand Award and Gold Award, DFA Design for Asia Awards 2021; The Plan Award 2021; Golden Trezzini 2021 (per Designboom 16 June 2021; Dezeen 2 October 2022; ArchDaily; Surface 2021; Hospitality Design feature; Neri&Hu official project page).
2. **ChenJiaShan Park Tea Lounge Renovation, Shanghai — Atelier Liu Yuyang Architects (ALYA), completed 2011.** Renovation of an unused park annex into a contemporary tea lounge in suburban Shanghai. Photography by Jeremy San (per ArchDaily 2014, *ChenJiaShan Park Tea Lounge Renovation / Atelier Liu Yuyang Architects*).
3. **Wuyuan Rd. Studio, Shanghai — Atelier Liu Yuyang Architects.** ALYA's own studio; "gray-colored river pebble terrazzo floor goes well with brass divider and margin"; "recycled Chinese Ash wood and copper hand pull-rod were used to construct their own door and window system" (per ArchDaily, *Wuyuan Rd. Studio / Atelier Liu Yuyang Architects*). Liu Yuyang holds an M.Arch from Harvard GSD (thesis under Rem Koolhaas, contributing to *Great Leap Forward*, Taschen 2001 — per Design Shanghai speaker bio for Yuyang Liu).
4. **XY Yunlu Hotel / Yun House, Yangshuo, Guangxi — Atelier Liu Yuyang Architects, completed 2019.** Boutique eco-resort on the Li River; nine renovated rammed-earth farmhouses + one new restaurant addition with steel frame + glass pivot doors against "rough-cut stone blocks, charcoal treated wooden louvers and terra-cotta roof tiles" (per Dezeen 14 October 2019). Awarded Best Sustainable Hospitality Project (China) 2019 (per BUILD Magazine).
5. **Tingtai Teahouse, Shanghai — Linehouse.** "Huge elevated boxes" form private tearooms inside a Shanghai warehouse — the contemporary industrial-meets-tradition mode of the genre (per Dezeen tea-houses tag).
6. **Atelier Deshaus Tea House, Shanghai West Bund.** A glass-walled tea pavilion at ~130 sq ft, with "a ring of benches suspended from the roof" beside a landscaped garden — extreme-restraint mode of the genre (per Dezeen tea-houses tag; My Modern Met feature).
7. **Bamboo Courtyard Teahouse, Yangzhou — Sun Wei / HWCD.** Floating teahouse in ShiQiao garden; basic square footprint "fragmented into small spaces to create an internal landscape area"; bamboo + brick construction; "thick brick wall retains heat in winter, reducing the dependency of mechanical heating and cooling" (per e-architect; Dezeen tea-houses tag).
8. **XinHeYe Tea House — Qicang Design, completed 2024.** Black-timber facade integrating with verdant surroundings; "earth-toned walls are paired with dark furniture crafted from wood and stone, while olive and yellow accents unify the palette" (per Designboom 29 December 2024).
9. **Tea House by Archi-Union Architects, Shanghai.** Constructed from salvaged parts of the original warehouse's collapsed roof; bottom-floor public tea house + curved staircase to upper-level intimate library/reading space; algorithmic form translated by hand to low-tech construction (per general Chinese tea-house genre coverage on Artsy and Archi-Union project listings).

## Common mistakes

1. **Imperial-pastiche overload — vermillion columns + gold-leaf panels + carved dragons.** The contemporary Guofeng canon (Neri&Hu, ALYA, Linehouse, Atelier Deshaus, Archi-Union, HWCD) is built on *restraint* against historic richness — Neri&Hu wraps a single ornate Qing structure in unornamented rammed concrete (per Dezeen 2 October 2022; Surface 2021). Stacking carved-dragon furniture on top of an already-rich timber hall reads as theme park, not architecture. Fix: pick *one* zone of historic richness (the timber hall, the carved screen) and let everything else be quiet.
2. **Polished marble or quartz as the dominant surface.** The genre's heavy mass is rammed concrete, rammed earth, or grey brick — tactile, age-tolerant. Polished stone reads as luxury hotel lobby. (Per Dezeen 2 October 2022 on Fuzhou's "rough, earth-coloured concrete, plaster and copper" secondary spaces.)
3. **Cool-white LED light.** Same logic as the Japanese tea-cafe — destroys the read of timber grain, rammed earth, and copper patina. Fix: clamp ≤2700K, layer multiple low-Kelvin sources rather than one bright ceiling.
4. **Skipping the courtyard / sky-well.** The genre's signature lighting and circulation move is the courtyard or sky-well bringing daylight into the heavy enclosure. Fuzhou's sky-wells "bring natural light into the depths of the enclosure" (per Surface 2021); Bamboo Courtyard's courtyard is the design's basic geometry (per e-architect). A render of a "Chinese tea-house" with no daylight aperture loses the genre's defining spatial move. Fix: cut a roof oculus or a courtyard, even a small one, and let directional sun fall onto a tea-table or single column.
5. **Walnut or warm-Western-wood timber.** The genre's wood is dark elm, dark pine, recycled ash, or preserved historic timber — not Western walnut. Cross-reference ALYA's recycled-ash detail (per ArchDaily Wuyuan Rd.). A walnut-paneled "Chinese tea-house" reads as Western mid-century with a tea-set on top.
6. **Treating bamboo as decorative wallpaper, not structure.** Bamboo Courtyard, XinHeYe, and Tea House by Archi-Union all use bamboo *structurally* or as substantial slat infill, not as painted-on motif (per e-architect; Designboom 29 December 2024). Fix: where bamboo is used, it should carry weight or define the volume (slat ceiling baffle, full bamboo screen, bamboo culm column array) — not appear as a printed graphic.
