# Style: Mediterranean (地中海)

> Sources: Studio Andrew Trotter project documentation (Masseria Moroseta, Casa Soleto, Casolare Scarani); Dezeen, "Studio Andrew Trotter refreshes 17th-century Casa Soleto in Puglia" (4 Sept 2023); Dezeen, "Studio Andrew Trotter transforms 19th-century school into family home in Puglia" (2 Mar 2023); Designboom, "andrew trotter's masseria moroseta stands among the picturesque olive groves of puglia" (5 Dec 2020); ArchDaily, "Masseria Moroseta / Studio Andrew Trotter"; Wallpaper*, "A dramatic Mallorcan house is a manifesto of contemporary Mediterranean living" (Casa Óculo by OHLAB); Brunet Arquitectes documentation of Son Vallfogó Gran (per Architectural Digest coverage). Specific named projects cited inline.
> Last updated: 2026-04-29

## Anchor description

Mediterranean residential design is the vernacular of the sun-bleached coastal house from Puglia to Mallorca to the Cyclades — thick lime-rendered walls, vaulted ceilings, terrazzo or stone floor, dark joinery against a pale plaster shell, and a controlled palette of "subtle earthy and greeny colours" tied to the local soil (per Marcelo Martínez, Studio Andrew Trotter, on Casa Soleto, Dezeen 2023). Studio Andrew Trotter's Masseria Moroseta (Ostuni, 2016) sets the contemporary residential benchmark: "wide insulating walls with calcio white lime wash … breathing walls and the vaulted ceiling, with modern materials and furnitures … light limestone floors cohabitate with graphic and colored concrete tiles, copper faucets, ancient wood, modern furniture, white tuff walls and dark windows and gates" (per ArchDaily, "Masseria Moroseta / Studio Andrew Trotter"). For a 3D scene this means: a one-meter-thick lime-washed wall as the dominant plane, terrazzo or limestone floor, a vaulted or barrel-arched ceiling, dark wood joinery, and an absence of synthetic finishes.

Practical rule: pick **lime-wash white (or warm Mallorcan stone) as dominant**, **terracotta or limestone as the floor / 30%**, and **one** chromatic accent — olive, ochre, or dark joinery. Casa Soleto explicitly uses **earthy + greeny** as a paired family rather than one pop color (per Dezeen, Casa Soleto 2023).

## Material vocabulary

### In

- **Lime plaster / lime wash on thick masonry walls.** Both Masseria Moroseta (calcio white lime wash on one-meter-thick walls — per ArchDaily) and Casa Soleto ("lime plasters to give a natural and raw feeling to the walls" — per Dezeen 2023) use lime as the defining wall finish. The texture, breathability, and chalk-matte sheen are not interchangeable with modern paint.
- **Terrazzo, *battuto alla veneziana*, and traditional stone-and-mortar floors.** Casa Soleto: "terrazzo floors – battuto alla veneziana – in the areas where new floors had to be made" (per Dezeen 2023). Casolare Scarani: "traditional stone flooring made from crushed rocks and mortar" (per Dezeen, 2 Mar 2023).
- **Light limestone floor planks.** Masseria Moroseta uses pale limestone as the room-to-room thread (per ArchDaily Masseria Moroseta).
- **Vaulted / barrel-arched ceilings.** Casolare Scarani: "the former school's vaulted ceilings were kept intact and covered, along with the walls, in a lime plaster created by a local artisan" (per Dezeen, 2 Mar 2023). Masseria Moroseta: "vaulted ceiling … modern materials and furnitures" (per ArchDaily).
- **Wooden windows and doors imitating originals; dark joinery as contrast against pale plaster.** Casa Soleto: "wooden windows and doors seeking to imitate the original ones, cast iron hardware and linen sofas" (per Dezeen 2023). Masseria Moroseta: "dark windows and gates" (per ArchDaily).
- **Cast iron hardware, copper plumbing fixtures, brass detail.** Masseria Moroseta: "copper faucets … ancient wood" (per ArchDaily). Casa Soleto: "cast iron hardware" (per Dezeen 2023).
- **Linen, raw cotton, undyed wool.** Casa Soleto interior: "linen sofas" (per Dezeen 2023). The textile vocabulary is undyed and natural-fibre.
- **Graphic and colored concrete tile (cementine).** Masseria Moroseta: "light limestone floors cohabitate with graphic and colored concrete tiles" (per ArchDaily). Used as inlaid pattern in entry / kitchen / bath, never wall-to-wall.

### Out

- **Drywall painted with synthetic / vinyl paint.** The whole point is the lime-wash texture; flat vinyl paint reads developer-spec, not Mediterranean.
- **Glossy reflective surfaces — laminate floors, lacquered cabinet fronts, mirror-polished metal.** Masseria Moroseta's "breathing walls" and tactile lime are explicitly the opposite of sealed gloss (per ArchDaily; Designboom 2020).
- **Chrome and stainless plumbing.** The published reference plumbing is *copper* (Masseria Moroseta) and *cast iron / brass* (Casa Soleto) — chrome breaks the read.
- **All-white sterile minimalism.** Casa Soleto: "Colours played a central role, as some make spaces feel light, others moody" (per Dezeen 2023). Mediterranean is not neutral-only; the earthy + greeny accents are mandatory, not optional.
- **Dark hardwood plank floor (oak, walnut).** This is the American / Scandinavian floor — Mediterranean's floor vocabulary is *stone, terrazzo, terracotta tile, or pale limestone*, not wood plank.

## Lighting profile

- **Kelvin range: 2400-2900K** — warm, but not as candle-orange as Scandinavian. The published Mediterranean photographic register is *sun-bleached daylight by day, warm tungsten lamp by night*. Casa Óculo's "natural brass details" against "thick, lime-rendered walls" photograph in mid-warm tungsten in the published Wallpaper* set (per Wallpaper*, "A dramatic Mallorcan house … Casa Óculo, OHLAB").
- **Daylight is the architectural protagonist.** Masseria Moroseta's one-meter-thick walls are punched with deep-set windows: "with a wall thickness of one meter (over three feet), and recycled insulation, very little air-conditioning is needed" (per ArchDaily). Render the deep window reveal as a directional shaft, not a flat plane.
- **Fixture style — the canonical references:**
  - **Pendant in opaline / hand-blown glass over the dining table.** The Casa Soleto and Casolare Scarani published interiors center the dining room around a single hand-blown glass pendant against the lime-plaster vault (per Dezeen 2023 image sets).
  - **Wall sconces in brass or aged iron, low-mounted.** Mediterranean walls are too thick and too tactile for recessed cans; surface-mount sconce is the period-correct fixture.
  - **Floor lamp with linen / paper drum shade.** The Casa Soleto sitting room places a floor lamp beside the linen sofa as the night reading light (per Dezeen 2023).
  - **Outdoor lantern at the porch / loggia.** The masseria/finca courtyard tradition; render as black metal cage or hand-blown amber glass.
- **Layer mix: daylight ambient + 1 dining pendant + 2-3 wall sconces + 1 floor lamp.** Roughly four-source per main room — fewer than Scandinavian's "many small caves of light," more than Muji's two-layer minimum.

## Camera bias

- **Strong directional sunlight through deep window reveals.** The signature Masseria Moroseta photographic move is the shaft of mid-morning light cutting across the lime-washed wall and limestone floor, framed by the deep one-meter window jamb (per Designboom 2020 image set; ArchDaily image set).
- **Eye height 1.4-1.6m**, the Western seated convention.
- **Vaulted ceiling visible in frame.** When rendering Mediterranean, *include the vault*. Casolare Scarani's published shots are deliberately framed to show the vaulted ceiling as the room's protagonist (per Dezeen 2 Mar 2023).
- **Vertical correction on; tolerate small handheld lens distortion that emphasizes the curvature of the vault.**
- **Foreground textile, midground table or seat group, background lime wall + arched window.** This is the Andrew Trotter editorial composition.

## Prop vocabulary

Build directly from the published Studio Andrew Trotter / Wallpaper* / Designboom interior inventory:

- **Linen sofa, slipcovered or upholstered in undyed natural fibre.** Casa Soleto: "linen sofas" explicitly (per Dezeen 2023).
- **Olive branch / single dried branch in a hand-thrown ceramic vessel.** The published Mediterranean prop convention; pairs with the olive grove context Masseria Moroseta inhabits (per Designboom 2020 — "stands among the picturesque olive groves of Puglia").
- **Hand-thrown ceramic pitcher, jug, and bowl in unglazed terracotta or matte slip.** The kitchen/dining surface convention; the brand-free, hand-made counterweight to the architectural plaster.
- **Woven rush / straw chair (Spanish / Italian vernacular — anea-seat ladderback, Andrea-Branzi-grade rush).** Casa Soleto's published rooms include rush-seat dining chairs as the regional vernacular (per Dezeen 2023 image set).
- **Cast iron candlestick or wrought-iron candelabra.** Reads "old house" rather than dressed; Casa Soleto's hardware vocabulary explicitly (per Dezeen 2023).
- **Single bold artwork — a contemporary canvas or framed sketch.** Studio Andrew Trotter's published interiors place one strong modern artwork against the lime-plaster field as the room's contemporary punctuation (per Casa Soleto and Casolare Scarani Dezeen image sets).
- **Open-shelved ceramics over a mortared stone counter.** The kitchen convention.

## Reference projects

1. **Studio Andrew Trotter, *Masseria Moroseta*, Ostuni, Italy, completed 2016 — ArchDaily, Designboom (5 Dec 2020), Anniversary Magazine**. Calcio white lime wash, vaulted ceilings, light limestone floors, graphic concrete tile inlay, copper faucets, dark window joinery. The contemporary Mediterranean residential benchmark.
2. **Studio Andrew Trotter + Marcelo Martínez, *Casa Soleto*, Puglia — Dezeen, 4 Sept 2023; Design Anthology UK feature**. 17th-century house renovated with lime plasters in "subtle earthy and greeny colours," terrazzo *battuto alla veneziana* floors, salvaged-monastery furniture, linen sofas, cast iron hardware.
3. **Studio Andrew Trotter, *Casolare Scarani*, Puglia — Dezeen, 2 Mar 2023**. 19th-century school converted to family home, with vaulted lime-plastered ceilings retained intact and traditional stone-and-mortar floors.
4. **OHLAB, *Casa Óculo*, Mallorca — Wallpaper*, "A dramatic Mallorcan house is a manifesto of contemporary Mediterranean living"**. Thick lime-rendered walls, stone floors, wooden carpentry, natural brass details. Useful as a contemporary-flat-roof Mallorcan counterpoint to the vaulted Puglia register.
5. **Brunet Arquitectes, *Son Vallfogó Gran*, Mallorca — Architectural Digest coverage**. Beige-reddish stone walls from the Llevant region, sloped Arabic-tiled roofs, wide porches — the Mallorcan vernacular reinterpreted with contemporary plan.
6. **Michael S. Smith, *Mallorcan villa*, published in The World of Interiors and later Architectural Digest**. The high-end Old World + Moorish/Moroccan crossover register; useful when a brief asks for "more decorated" Mediterranean than the Andrew Trotter pared-back vocabulary.

## Common mistakes

1. **Stucco-textured drywall instead of real lime plaster.** A sprayed acrylic stucco texture does not read like lime. Andrew Trotter is explicit that the lime plaster's "natural and raw feeling" is the point and that it was made by a local artisan (per Dezeen Casolare Scarani 2023 and Casa Soleto 2023). Fix: render with subtle non-uniform color variation, visible trowel direction, and a chalk-matte BRDF — not a uniform tinted drywall.
2. **Cobalt-blue-and-white "Santorini cliché."** The published Mediterranean register from Andrew Trotter / OHLAB / Brunet is *not* primary-blue-and-white. It is earthy + greeny + pale stone (per Dezeen Casa Soleto, 2023; Wallpaper* Casa Óculo). Fix: replace cobalt accents with olive green, ochre, sienna, or dark charred-wood joinery.
3. **Wood plank floor in a lime-plaster room.** The Mediterranean floor is stone, terrazzo, or terracotta tile (per Masseria Moroseta limestone, Casa Soleto *battuto alla veneziana*, Casolare Scarani crushed-rock-and-mortar). A wide oak plank floor breaks the read into Scandinavian or American Classic. Fix: use stone / terrazzo / pale limestone as the floor primitive.
4. **Bright recessed downlights replacing wall sconces and pendants.** The published Mediterranean lighting is *surface-mounted and warm* — pendant + sconce + lantern (per Casa Soleto and Masseria Moroseta image sets). Recessed cans flatten the deep-window-reveal architectural drama. Fix: keep at least one pendant per primary room and skip the can grid.
5. **Contemporary glossy plumbing fixtures.** Masseria Moroseta uses copper; Casa Soleto uses cast iron. Polished chrome breaks the read. Fix: spec unlacquered copper or aged brass for taps and visible plumbing.
