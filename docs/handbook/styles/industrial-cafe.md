# Style: Industrial Cafe (工业风咖啡馆)

> Sources: Dezeen 9 August 2019 ("Schemata Architects stacks up red bricks inside South Korea's first Blue Bottle Coffee cafe"); Azure Magazine feature "An Industrial Palette Defines Seoul's Blue Bottle Coffee"; Leibal feature on Blue Bottle Coffee Seongsu Cafe; Dezeen Awards 2020 Longlist entry for Schemata Architects (Blue Bottle Seongsu); Designboom 2 April 2019 ("schemata architects tucks eleventh blue bottle cafe under 100-year-old bridge in tokyo"); Studio Butch project page for Stumptown Coffee Infiniti / 11W cafe, Portland; Daily Coffee News 15 May 2024 ("Stumptown Coffee Opens Anew in Downtown Portland"); Czopek Design Studio project page for Stumptown Coffee Roasters HQ, Portland; Works Partnership Architecture project page for Stumptown Coffee Roasters HQ; Interior Design magazine ("All the Buzz: Stumptown Comes to Greenwich Village"); Architizer + Sprudge feature on Intelligentsia Coffee Silverlake by Bestor Architecture; LA Weekly 2010 ("Intelligentsia Coffeehouse Opens in Pasadena"); Sprudge 2022 ("Build-Outs Of Coffee: The Illumination Bar By Intelligentsia In Pasadena, CA") on Prospect Studio (Danny Wicke). Specific named projects cited inline.
> Last updated: 2026-04-29

## Anchor description

The industrial cafe — distinct from the residential industrial-loft — is a public, high-throughput hospitality space dropped into a heavy structural envelope (a Seongsu factory, a Brooklyn warehouse, a Pasadena 1886 commercial frontage) where the building's working-class material vocabulary is preserved as the design's main asset (per Dezeen 9 August 2019, "Schemata Architects stacks up red bricks inside South Korea's first Blue Bottle Coffee cafe"). The defining moves: a tightly limited material palette of concrete + brick + stainless steel + reclaimed wood, a single visible piece of working production equipment (a probat roaster, a La Marzocco espresso head, a cupping table), large daylight glazing, and warm-amber low-pendant fixtures that push the eye to the bar (per Azure Magazine on Blue Bottle Coffee Seongsu; per Sprudge 2022 on the Illumination Bar by Intelligentsia). For a 3D scene this means: exposed concrete or brick walls, a stainless-steel + reclaimed-wood bar as the sculptural hero, a Probat or comparable roaster visible as theatre, 6-12 filament pendants, and a daylight-flooded window wall — *not* a residential loft layout with sofas and floor lamps.

Practical rule for an AI applying this palette: pick **one** dominant shell (raw concrete OR exposed brick — not both equally), **one** secondary timber (reclaimed Douglas fir, oak, or cedar), and **one** accent that is *either* stainless steel (the Blue Bottle / Schemata move) *or* a single saturated wall color (the Intelligentsia cornflower, the Stumptown deep green). Mixing both stainless steel and a saturated paint accent at full strength reads cluttered.

## Material vocabulary

### In

- **Raw concrete (unpainted, unsealed where code allows).** Blue Bottle Seongsu uses concrete as the *majority* surface material across all three floors (per Dezeen 9 August 2019; Azure Magazine on Blue Bottle Seongsu). Concrete reads as honest in industrial-cafe; sealed-and-painted concrete reads as office.
- **Brick — Seongsu red, reclaimed, "freely stacked yet discretely mortared."** Schemata at Blue Bottle Seongsu describes the brick as "appear[ing] freely stacked yet discretely mortared" and used to build "minimalist sculptures" / built-in furniture (per Dezeen 9 August 2019 on Blue Bottle Seongsu; Azure Magazine feature). Brick functions both as building shell and as solid-mass furniture.
- **Stainless steel — counters, stair balustrades, doors, structural columns.** Schemata's Blue Bottle Seongsu deploys stainless steel as a near-universal accent surface, taking advantage of the fact that "stainless steel is less expensive in Korea than in Japan, [so] we used stainless steel to make various elements including side walls of counters, stairs, handrails, and doors" (per Dezeen 9 August 2019 quoting Schemata).
- **Reclaimed wood from the building's own past.** Intelligentsia Pasadena's bar is "Douglas fir reclaimed from the 1886 building" (per LA Weekly 2010); Stumptown Portland HQ uses "reclaimed cedar" for the exterior (per Works Partnership Architecture project page). Reclaim *from the building*, not generic vintage wood.
- **Working roastery equipment as visible programme.** Schemata's Blue Bottle Seongsu places "a roastery, a storage room for coffee beans and an area dedicated to cupping" on the ground floor "fronted by glass, allowing passersby on the street a glimpse at the brand's coffee-making process" (per Dezeen 9 August 2019 / Azure Magazine on Blue Bottle Seongsu). Stumptown Portland HQ co-locates "the roasting facility … office space and production and training areas" under one roof (per Czopek Design Studio project page; Works Partnership Architecture). The roaster *is* a fixture for industrial cafe.
- **Floor-to-ceiling glass to the street.** Blue Bottle Seongsu fronts the ground level with full glass for street-side roastery visibility (per Dezeen 9 August 2019). Industrial-cafe lighting depends on this large daylight gap; treat it as a hard requirement, not a stylistic option.
- **Reclaimed church pews / vintage commercial seating.** Intelligentsia Pasadena added "church pews from Cairo, Illinois" as additional seating (per LA Weekly 2010 on Intelligentsia Pasadena). Borrow this typology — pews, school benches, factory stools — rather than off-the-shelf restaurant chairs.
- **Saturated paint accent on a single architectural surface (wall or ceiling).** Stumptown 11W Portland: "a green-painted ceiling intended to reduce the visual clutter of exposed ductwork, conduit, and other elements in the raw space" (per Studio Butch project page; Daily Coffee News 15 May 2024). The painted ceiling is a visual-quieting tool, not just a brand move.
- **Custom ceiling fixtures referencing craft objects.** Stumptown 11W: "a large custom ceiling fixture referencing bento boxes was designed to hang over the main seating area opposite the coffee counter, providing diffused lighting for cafe guests" (per Studio Butch / Daily Coffee News 15 May 2024). One bespoke fixture per cafe is the editorial tell.

### Out

- **Soft residential carpet, large floor rugs, fabric ottomans.** None of the named industrial-cafe projects use this vocabulary. Industrial cafes are throughput hospitality — they need wipeable floors and light-stacking seating. Carpet reads "WeWork lounge," not industrial cafe.
- **Decorative reproduction "Edison" bulbs hung as a chandelier cluster of 30+.** While Edison-style filament bulbs do appear in the genre, the named published examples use restraint: Stumptown 11W has *one* custom bento-box fixture (per Studio Butch / Daily Coffee News 15 May 2024); Intelligentsia Pasadena's original used "bare lights strung from the rafters" sparsely (per LA Weekly 2010). 30-bulb chandelier clusters read as 2014 cliché.
- **Heavy ornamental tin ceilings, faux-distressed shiplap.** None of the named industrial-cafe projects use these moves; they read as "rustic restaurant" not industrial cafe. The published mode is *real* exposed structure with a single painted-ceiling or fixture intervention.
- **Couches and floor lamps as if it were a residential loft.** This is the explicit boundary with `industrial-loft.md`. Industrial cafe seating is bench, stool, pew, or hard chair — not a Chesterfield. Lighting is pendant or fixture, not a floor lamp.
- **Marble countertop on the bar.** None of the named industrial cafes use marble; Blue Bottle Seongsu's bar is stainless steel (per Dezeen 9 August 2019); Intelligentsia Pasadena's bar is reclaimed Douglas fir (per LA Weekly 2010); Stumptown 11W's bar is wrapped in "deep green, textured tile" (per Studio Butch). Marble reads luxe, not industrial-cafe.

## Lighting profile

- **Kelvin range: 2700-3000K with very strong daylight.** None of the named projects state Kelvin numerically, but the published image set reads as warm tungsten / warm filament against daylight — never the candle-warm 1800-2200K of the speakeasy / hospitality range. Industrial cafe lighting needs to support reading and laptop work in many of the named locations. Set 2700-3000K as the LED range, daylight at 5500K through the front glass.
- **Daylight is the primary key.** Schemata's Blue Bottle Seongsu specifically locates the cafe in a basement *because* "part of the first floor slab was punched out to create a cafe with a double height ceiling in the basement" — an intentional daylight-shaft strategy (per Azure Magazine; Dezeen 9 August 2019). For an industrial-cafe render, design a top-light or front-glass daylight strategy first, fixtures second.
- **Pendant lights at bar height (1.5-1.8m above counter).** Industrial cafe lighting clusters concentrate over the bar — the brand's working surface — rather than over seating zones. A row of 3-6 filament or diffused-glass pendants over the bar is the canonical move.
- **One bespoke statement fixture.** Stumptown 11W's bento-box pendant (per Studio Butch / Daily Coffee News 15 May 2024) is the rule, not the exception — published industrial cafes earn their editorial coverage by having *one* designed-not-bought fixture.
- **Ambient lux ≈300-500, accent lux ≈80-150 over bar.** Higher than restaurant or hospitality, lower than retail or office; this is a functional working-cafe environment with people on laptops 4+ hours at a time. Cross-ref `lighting.md` baseline.
- **Avoid red / amber gels.** Industrial cafe is *not* speakeasy. Keep saturated colored light out except as small signage glow.

## Camera bias

- **24-35mm equivalent, eye height 1.5-1.6m, anchored on the bar.** The published Schemata Blue Bottle Seongsu image set centers the bar as the composition anchor with concrete + brick + stainless steel reading in equal contrast (per Dezeen 9 August 2019; Azure Magazine on Blue Bottle Seongsu). The Sprudge / Studio Butch coverage of Stumptown and Intelligentsia consistently uses bar-centered framing.
- **Verticals straightened.** Cross-ref `camera.md`.
- **Three-layer stack: foreground guest table, midground bar with barista, background roaster / wall art / production gear.** Schemata's Blue Bottle Seongsu design philosophy is "to make the corporate offices, roasting operations and cafe all feel closely and seamlessly integrated" (per Dezeen 9 August 2019; Azure on Blue Bottle Seongsu) — the camera's job is to show all three programmes in one frame.
- **Daylight key, with the bar slightly underexposed.** The published Blue Bottle Seongsu basement shots use the punched-slab daylight as the key with the bar reading 1-2 stops darker, allowing the stainless steel to glow from reflected daylight rather than from direct fixture light (per Azure Magazine on Blue Bottle Seongsu).
- **Counter-stool eye-line as a secondary framing.** Pasadena Intelligentsia's "high tables in the front" (per LA Weekly 2010) photograph well from a counter-stool eye-line of about 1.1m, capturing the bar from a customer's perspective.

## Prop vocabulary

Industrial cafe prop checklist, drawn from named published projects:

- **A visible Probat / commercial-grade roaster — drum-roaster silhouette.** Schemata Blue Bottle Seongsu's ground floor is roastery + cupping room *as the public façade* (per Dezeen 9 August 2019; Azure Magazine on Blue Bottle Seongsu); Stumptown HQ is roastery + offices + training in one envelope (per Czopek / Works Partnership Architecture project pages). For a render, place the roaster behind glass with the roast log book on a wall clipboard.
- **A La Marzocco / Synesso espresso machine, custom-finished.** Stumptown 11W: "the espresso machine is specced with a custom finish inspired by the concentric curves of a Japanese zen garden" (per Studio Butch / Daily Coffee News 15 May 2024). The espresso machine is the second-most-photographed object after the bar itself.
- **A cupping table with bowls, spoons, and grinder samples.** Schemata Blue Bottle Seongsu has "an area dedicated to cupping" (per Dezeen 9 August 2019). A cupping table reads as serious-coffee instantly.
- **Brick-built furniture or counter mass.** Schemata at Blue Bottle Seongsu "made some pieces of furniture out of bricks" (per Dezeen 9 August 2019; Azure Magazine). A solid brick service mass is more characteristic of industrial-cafe than any wooden cabinet.
- **Reclaimed-wood church pews / school benches as banquette seating.** Intelligentsia Pasadena's "church pews from Cairo, Illinois" (per LA Weekly 2010). One pew run + a row of stools is the canonical industrial-cafe seating layout.
- **Bags of single-origin coffee on shelves, brand-colored.** Intelligentsia Pasadena: "coffee beans in bags colored a Soviet-era red stacked on the shelves" (per LA Weekly 2010). Stack coffee bags as architectural texture, not as warehouse overflow.
- **Custom ceiling fixture in a bespoke material reference.** Stumptown 11W's "bento-box" pendant (per Studio Butch / Daily Coffee News 15 May 2024). Render one bespoke fixture per cafe.
- **Backlit hand-formed plaster wall installation.** Stumptown 11W: "a swooping backlit installation with a hand-formed plaster fan motif rises behind the simple wood banquette in the same area" (per Studio Butch / Daily Coffee News 15 May 2024). One sculptural wall feature per cafe, modest in scale.
- **A kintsugi-style ceramic art piece on a wall.** Stumptown 11W centers "a centerpiece art piece … created in the style of kintsugi—the Japanese art of mending broken objects with gold, celebrating imperfection rather than concealing it. The ceramic disc features gold lines arranged to resemble a simplified map of Portland" (per Studio Butch / Daily Coffee News 15 May 2024). Treat the *one* feature artwork as part of the prop list.

## Reference projects

1. **Schemata Architects (Jo Nagasaka), *Blue Bottle Coffee Seongsu Cafe*, Seoul — Dezeen 9 August 2019 ("Schemata Architects stacks up red bricks inside South Korea's first Blue Bottle Coffee cafe"); Azure Magazine; Leibal; Dezeen Awards 2020 Longlist.** Three floors (basement cafe + ground roastery + second-floor office) inside a Seongsu industrial four-storey + basement; concrete + Seongsu red brick + stainless steel; brick-built furniture; punched slab for double-height basement daylight; floor-to-ceiling street glass on the roastery level. The reference project for industrial-cafe at scale.
2. **Schemata Architects, *Blue Bottle Coffee Kanda Manseibashi*, Tokyo — Designboom 2 April 2019 ("schemata architects tucks eleventh blue bottle cafe under 100-year-old bridge in tokyo").** Cited as the lineage parent for the Schemata Blue Bottle methodology — Schemata had already designed multiple Tokyo Blue Bottle interiors, including Roppongi (basswood-lined) and Kiyosumi-Shirakawa (plant-filled atrium), before bringing the methodology to Seongsu (per Dezeen 9 August 2019). Useful as a Japan-set counter-reference.
3. **Works Partnership Architecture + Czopek Design Studio, *Stumptown Coffee Roasters HQ*, Portland — Czopek Design Studio project page; Works Partnership Architecture project page.** 40,000 sq ft adaptive reuse co-locating the Oregon roastery, national corporate offices, and production training under one roof; reclaimed cedar exterior wrap; "state of the art sustainable strategies for heat recovery and roasting functions"; existing rustic-finish industrial shell preserved (per Works Partnership Architecture project page; Czopek Design Studio project page). The reference for "real working roastery + cafe" industrial-cafe at headquarters scale.
4. **Studio Butch + Green Rabbet Builders, *Stumptown Coffee 11W Cafe*, Downtown Portland — Studio Butch project page; Daily Coffee News 15 May 2024 ("Stumptown Coffee Opens Anew in Downtown Portland").** New-build cafe inside the 11W mixed-use building at SW 11th + SW Washington; matte-finish natural oak floors / wall cladding / furnishings; deep green textured-tile counter wrap with green-painted ceiling for visual quieting; bento-box ceiling fixture; backlit plaster fan-motif wall; kintsugi ceramic disc. The reference for *Japandi-leaning* industrial cafe — useful as a polished-end cousin of the rougher Schemata mode.
5. **Bestor Architecture, *Intelligentsia Coffee Silverlake*, Los Angeles — Architizer project page; Sprudge feature.** West Coast flagship for the Chicago-based importer-roaster; Donald Judd-derived "plywood box" minimalism mashed with strikingly bold tile; designed as "a public living room defined by rich material and social qualities" (per Architizer Bestor Architecture / Intelligentsia Silverlake project page). The reference for design-forward LA industrial cafe.
6. **Prospect Studio (Danny Wicke), *Intelligentsia Pasadena — The Illumination Bar*, Pasadena — Sprudge 2022 ("Build-Outs Of Coffee: The Illumination Bar"); LA Weekly 2010 ("Intelligentsia Coffeehouse Opens in Pasadena") for the original 2010 build.** 2,000 sq ft. Original 2010: "dark cornflower blue" walls + reclaimed Douglas fir bar from the 1886 building + church pews from Cairo, Illinois + Soviet-red coffee bags as merch wall. 2022 reimagining: "playful experimentation workshop" with "intentional, flexible workspace that allows guests to have full transparency" (per Sprudge 2022 on the Illumination Bar by Prospect Studio / Danny Wicke). Reference for evolution of an industrial cafe across a decade.

## Common mistakes

1. **Building an industrial-cafe scene as if it were an industrial-loft residence.** This is the single most common error and the boundary case `industrial-loft.md` exists to disambiguate. The named industrial-cafe projects use *bar-centered* layouts with bench/stool/pew seating, large daylight glazing, visible production equipment, and pendant clusters over the bar — not sofa pits, floor lamps, or coffee tables (per Dezeen 9 August 2019 on Blue Bottle Seongsu; per LA Weekly 2010 on Intelligentsia Pasadena). Fix: if the rendered scene has a sofa, rugs, and floor lamps, the genre is wrong.
2. **30+ Edison-bulb chandelier clusters.** None of the named published industrial cafes use 30-bulb pendant clusters; the published mode is *one* bespoke fixture (Stumptown 11W's bento box, per Studio Butch / Daily Coffee News 15 May 2024) plus a sparse row over the bar (Intelligentsia Pasadena's "bare lights strung from the rafters," per LA Weekly 2010). Fix: cap pendant count at 6-12, and make at least one fixture bespoke.
3. **Faux-distressed shiplap, fake "industrial" pipe-leg tables.** The named projects use *real* reclaimed material from a *real* historic source — Intelligentsia Pasadena's Douglas fir bar from "the 1886 building" (per LA Weekly 2010), Stumptown HQ's "reclaimed cedar to create a brand identity" (per Works Partnership Architecture project page). Fix: render reclaimed wood with grain, nail holes, paint history, and surface wear; pipe-leg furniture from a catalog reads as 2015 trend.
4. **Marble countertop on the bar.** Industrial cafe bars are stainless steel (Blue Bottle Seongsu, per Dezeen 9 August 2019), reclaimed wood (Intelligentsia Pasadena, per LA Weekly 2010), or saturated tile (Stumptown 11W, per Studio Butch / Daily Coffee News 15 May 2024). A marble bar pulls the genre toward hospitality / luxe and away from the "real working roastery" identity.
5. **No visible roaster or production equipment.** The defining published industrial-cafe move is to put production *on view*: Schemata makes the Seongsu roastery visible from the street through floor-to-ceiling glass (per Dezeen 9 August 2019 on Blue Bottle Seongsu); Stumptown HQ co-locates roastery + offices + training (per Works Partnership Architecture project page). A render without a roaster, a cupping table, or at least a working espresso machine in the frame loses the genre's anchor.
