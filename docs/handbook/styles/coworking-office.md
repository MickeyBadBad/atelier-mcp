# Style: Coworking Office (联合办公)

> Sources: Dezeen 14 February 2017 ("SelgasCano completes plant-filled co-working space inside Lisbon market hall for Second Home"); ArchDaily project page for Second Home London Office by SelgasCano (2014, photography Iwan Baan); Wallpaper* 14 May 2018 ("Bjarke Ingels joins WeWork as chief architect"); Architect Magazine and Architectural Record coverage May 2018 of Bjarke Ingels at WeWork; Dezeen 1 April 2019 ("Michel Rojkind joins Bjarke Ingels as architect at The We Company"); Dezeen 4 December 2017 ("Female co-working club The Wing opens in New York loft"); Dezeen 28 February 2018 ("Female co-working club The Wing's third outpost occupies old Brooklyn paper factory"); Office Snapshots feature on The Wing San Francisco (2018); Metropolis profile of Chiara de Rege + Alda Ly for The Wing; Dezeen 20 March 2022 ("Ten sleek co-working spaces that provide an escape from home working"). Specific named projects cited inline.
> Last updated: 2026-04-29

## Anchor description

Coworking interior design is a hybrid: the structural shell and amenity density of a hospitality interior, the desk count of a Class-B office, and the visual identity of a consumer-brand flagship — all packed into a single floor plate that has to feel inhabited at 7am and at 9pm (per Wallpaper* 14 May 2018, "Bjarke Ingels joins WeWork as chief architect," paraphrasing Ingels on "buildings, campuses, and neighborhoods" as the brief). The signature design move across the most-published examples is to inhabit a heavy structural envelope — a 19th-century market hall, a 1900s paper factory, a Lord & Taylor flagship, a Bush Terminal manufacturing building — and then layer a fully-furnished living-room language *over* it, using plants, color, vintage furniture, and curved glazing to break the openness into rooms-without-doors (per Dezeen 14 February 2017 on Second Home Lisboa). For a 3D scene this means: an exposed structural shell at one or two stories, a serpentine or wavy hero desk that meanders rather than gridding, 100+ pot plants, mismatched mid-century chairs, curved glass meeting-room pods, and one signature brand color carried through paint and upholstery.

Practical rule for an AI applying this palette: pick **one** dominant shell (white-painted brick OR raw concrete OR open market-hall steel), **one** secondary oak floor or vintage-rug warmth, and **one** brand accent that *carries through* paint, upholstery, and a single hero object (the table, the conversation pit, the foyer). Mixing two strong brand accents at equal weight reads as "agency lobby," not coworking. Plants count as an accent — 1,000+ specimens *is* the SelgasCano move (per Dezeen 14 February 2017).

## Material vocabulary

### In

- **Exposed structural columns, beams, ductwork.** Universal across the named projects. The Wing DUMBO leaves "columns, beams and ductwork exposed" (per Dezeen 28 February 2018); Second Home Lisboa is "left open plan to show off the columns and iron windows of the historic market hall, which was built in 1892" (per Dezeen 14 February 2017 on Second Home Lisboa, citing the Mercado da Ribeira context). The shell is the design's biggest asset.
- **Curved glazing forming meeting-room pods within the open plan.** SelgasCano's signature: "four private meeting rooms surrounded by curved glazed partitions, another hallmark of their office design projects" (per ArchDaily project page for Second Home London Office). For coworking renders, isolate enclosed spaces with curved glass rather than orthogonal drywall.
- **Serpentine / wavy communal desk as the room's organizing object.** Second Home Lisboa's "tables, measuring 70 metres by 10 metres and designed by SelgasCano, meander through the shared workspace and are intended to encourage members to collaborate" (per Dezeen 14 February 2017). Cano explains the meander as a way of giving "members their own area of private space to focus on their work whilst keeping a sense of community." Use a winding hero desk as the central organizing geometry, never a perfect grid.
- **Methacrylate, fibreglass, kronospan as the SelgasCano material set.** Project-specification material list for Second Home Lisboa explicitly: "methacralyte, fibreglass, and kronospan" alongside radiant heat flooring and low-energy LED bulbs (per ArchDaily project page for Second Home London / Second Home Lisboa specifications). These are the colorful, lightweight, plant-compatible materials of the SelgasCano coworking vocabulary.
- **Tinted concrete floors as a brand surface.** The Wing DUMBO "poured pink concrete for a distinctive flourish," carrying the brand color *into* the slab itself rather than as paint over it (per Dezeen 28 February 2018). For coworking with a strong brand, pour the brand color into the floor.
- **Mid-century vintage furniture, mismatched.** Second Home Lisboa includes "500 different chairs and lamps … mid-century original chairs and lamps, the Howe 40/4 swivel chair designed by David Rowland, and SelgasCano-designed tables" (per ArchDaily project page for Second Home Lisboa specifications). The mismatch is doctrinal — every chair is different on purpose.
- **Hydroponic plant walls as room divider.** "The plants are architectural dividers. The plants are the walls, sound barriers and for the creative psychological environment" — SelgasCano + Sam Aldenton (Second Home cofounder) on the SelgasCano method (per Dezeen 14 February 2017). A coworking render without dense plant cover at the room's edge reads as a generic startup office.
- **Velvet conversation pits, perforated metal, frosty acrylic, brass sconces, cane.** The Wing SoHo's mid-detail vocabulary: "furnishings made of perforated and twisted metal, frosty acrylic, slick brass, and cane … birch panels that line the walls" plus "brass sconces to reflect the natural light from the large rooflight" (per Dezeen 4 December 2017). Use this as the high-trim accent set when the project is brand-forward.

### Out

- **Cubicle walls, modular partition systems, carpet tiles in 60×60cm grid.** None of the named published coworking interiors use this vocabulary; the entire premise of post-2014 coworking is the *removal* of the cube farm. Second Home is explicitly described as standing in for "an inherited idea of one single open space" (per Lucia Cano via Dezeen 14 February 2017 / ArchDaily Second Home Lisboa project page).
- **Cool-white 4000K+ ceiling-grid LED panels.** Second Home Lisboa's specification calls out "low energy consumption LED light-bulbs" used as scattered points, not a ceiling grid (per ArchDaily project page); the published image set uniformly reads warm. Cool-white grid lighting reads as "1990s call center," explicitly the look the genre rejects.
- **Open-plan-as-bench-rows.** Even the open plan in named projects is *broken* — by curved glass pods (SelgasCano), conversation pits (The Wing DUMBO), or pink-painted archways (The Wing SoHo). Long parallel benches without enclaves read as a workspace clone, not coworking.
- **Single-color "corporate beige" interiors.** The Wing's whole brief is to be "feminine without being girly, sophisticated but nothing like the leather-and-billiards men's clubs of yore" (per Metropolis profile of Chiara de Rege). The genre demands at least one saturated brand color carried through the scheme.
- **Decorative-only plants — 3 small succulents on a shelf.** The published quantum is 1,000+ plants (Second Home London) or 1,200 (Lisboa) (per ArchDaily Second Home London / Dezeen 14 February 2017 Lisboa). For a coworking render, plants are architecture, not garnish; budget at least 30-50 specimens for a 100m² area or it reads off.

## Lighting profile

- **Kelvin range: 2700-3000K with strong daylight contribution.** None of the named projects specify Kelvin numerically, but the published images uniformly read warm-but-not-candle: an upper-warm range that supports task work without going hospitality-dim. Second Home Lisboa relies on "skylights running the whole length of the building along the ridge of the roof" plus "low energy consumption LED light-bulbs" as point sources (per ArchDaily Second Home Lisboa specification page). The Wing SoHo has "natural light from the large rooflight" reflected by "brass sconces" (per Dezeen 4 December 2017). For a coworking render, set daylight as the dominant key and warm 2700-3000K LED as fill.
- **Skylights / clerestory daylight as the primary key.** The hard pattern: every named coworking source project has a major top-light strategy. Second Home Lisboa: skylights along the roof ridge + bay windows (per ArchDaily). The Wing SoHo: large rooflight (per Dezeen 4 December 2017). Industry City coworking: "spacious, light-filled lounges" exploiting industrial-shed glazing (per industrycity.com Cowork IC brochure). If your render is of a coworking floor with no daylight strategy visible, it reads wrong.
- **Mismatched-vintage table and floor lamps as a layer.** Second Home Lisboa specifies "500 different chairs and lamps … locally sourced vintage lighting" (per ArchDaily project page). The Wing's overall furnishing brief includes "an eclectic range of chairs and sofas in contrasting pastel and bold tones" (per Dezeen 28 February 2018). Scatter 8-15 vintage / mid-century lamps across the floor plate as accent layer.
- **Brass / wall-sconce accent layer at desk-zone height.** The Wing SoHo's "brass sconces to reflect the natural light from the large rooflight" (per Dezeen 4 December 2017) is the canonical brand-coworking sconce move. Place sconces at 1.6-1.8m above floor along feature walls.
- **Ambient lux ≈150-300, accent lux ≈40-80.** Higher than residential or hospitality because there is real laptop work happening (cross-reference `lighting.md` baseline). Coworking sits in the warm-but-bright corner of the matrix.

## Camera bias

- **24-35mm equivalent, eye height 1.6-1.7m, deep frame.** The published Second Home image set (Iwan Baan for SelgasCano) consistently uses the wavy 70m communal table as a leading line that recedes through the frame, with the curved glass pods clustered in the midground and plants framing both edges (per ArchDaily Second Home London image set, photography Iwan Baan; Dezeen 14 February 2017 image set on Second Home Lisboa).
- **Verticals straightened.** Same project-wide convention; cross-ref `camera.md`.
- **Foreground plant cluster, midground active workers, background structural shell.** The three-layer staging that recurs across Second Home, The Wing DUMBO, and Cowork IC photography. A coworking render without visible *people* reads as an unfinished construction shot — coworking interiors are validated by occupation, not by emptiness.
- **Wide horizontal framing for the open-plan hero, 3:2 portrait for pod / sconce vignettes.** The Iwan Baan Second Home set works in 16:9-ish horizontal for the long table, then portrait for the curved-glass pod close-ups — both crops are part of the deliverable (per ArchDaily Second Home London image set and Dezeen 14 February 2017 image set).

## Prop vocabulary

Coworking interior prop checklist, drawn from named published projects:

- **A 50-70m winding communal table, designed in-house.** The defining SelgasCano gesture: "tables, measuring 70 metres by 10 metres and designed by SelgasCano, meander through the shared workspace" (per Dezeen 14 February 2017 on Second Home Lisboa). Even at smaller scales, prefer a serpentine or organic-edge desk over a straight bench.
- **30-1,200 hydroponic plants ringing every desk-edge.** "1,000 hydroponically cultivated plants line the edges of every office" (Second Home London, per ArchDaily project page); "1,200" at Lisboa (per ArchDaily). At a small render scale, scale linearly: 1 plant per 1m of desk perimeter is the SelgasCano density.
- **Curved-glass meeting pods in 2-4-person and 6-10-person sizes.** Second Home consistently uses "curved glazed partitions" for "private meeting rooms" inside the open plan (per ArchDaily Second Home London project page).
- **Mid-century chairs, mismatched, never a single SKU.** "500 different chairs" at Second Home Lisboa, including "the Howe 40/4 swivel chair designed by David Rowland" plus "mid-century original chairs and lamps" (per ArchDaily Second Home Lisboa specification page).
- **Conversation pit / built-in banquette in a saturated brand color.** The Wing DUMBO's "green velvet … conversation pit" (per Dezeen 28 February 2018) is the canonical brand-coworking soft-program element. Drop one into the floor plan for any branded-coworking render.
- **Pink granite or terrazzo central table.** The Wing DUMBO atrium has a "pink granite dining table … as a central meeting place" (per Dezeen 28 February 2018); The Wing SoHo uses "small, custom-made terrazzo tables" (per Dezeen 4 December 2017). Terrazzo or saturated stone reads as coworking-club, not corporate.
- **Decorative wallpaper or wall-coverings in a private meeting space.** The Wing DUMBO sets up "a private meeting space like a dining room, featuring a table and chairs, pink wallpaper decorated by black line drawings, and a thickly woven rug" (per Dezeen 28 February 2018). One wallpapered enclave per floor adds the residential-feeling enclave needed.
- **Color-organized floor-to-ceiling bookshelves.** The Wing Flatiron is anchored by "floor-to-ceiling bookshelves organized by color and stocked with books from a range of women and nonbinary writers" (per Metropolis profile of Chiara de Rege). Treat the library as a hero wall.
- **Phone booths / soundproof single-occupancy pods.** Standard in named published coworking floor plates including Camp David at Industry City (per industrycity.com / Cowork IC brochure: "soundproof phone booths"). Add 2-4 per 100 desks.

## Reference projects

1. **SelgasCano (Jose Selgas + Lucia Cano), *Second Home Lisboa*, Mercado da Ribeira, Lisbon — Dezeen 14 February 2017; ArchDaily project page; photography Iwan Baan.** 1,115 m² inside an 1892 market hall; ironwork painted mint, navy, yellow; foyer in Yves Klein blue; 70m × 10m wavy SelgasCano-designed communal table; 1,200 plants; methacrylate / fibreglass / kronospan material set; radiant floor heating + greenhouse-derived natural ventilation; €1.3 million project cost. The reference project for biophilic coworking.
2. **SelgasCano, *Second Home Spitalfields*, London — ArchDaily project page (built 2014, photography Iwan Baan).** Original Second Home; 1,000 hydroponic plants line every desk-edge; curved-glass private meeting rooms; the prototype that the Lisboa and West London outposts evolved from. Useful to cite when the user is in a tighter floor plate than Lisboa's market hall.
3. **Alda Ly Architecture + Chiara de Rege, *The Wing Flatiron*, New York — Metropolis profile of de Rege + Ly; Pentagram identity work.** First Wing location; pale-pink walls, wingback chairs, color-organized bookshelves; the prototype that established "Wing pink" as a brand identity carried through paint, fabric, and concrete. The reference for brand-driven women-focused coworking.
4. **Alda Ly Architecture + Chiara de Rege, *The Wing SoHo*, New York — Dezeen 4 December 2017.** Stripped to existing wood floorboards + brick walls painted white; pale-green benches with custom terrazzo tables; brass sconces; birch wall panels; archways "painted in different shades of pink." The reference for refined-loft coworking.
5. **Alda Ly Architecture + Chiara de Rege, *The Wing DUMBO*, Brooklyn — Dezeen 28 February 2018.** Old Brooklyn paper factory; columns / beams / ductwork exposed; *pink concrete poured floor*; pink granite central table; pale oak flooring; green velvet conversation pit; pink-wallpapered private dining-style meeting room. The reference for industrial-shell coworking with full brand carry-through.
6. **Bjarke Ingels Group + The We Company, *WeGrow micro-school*, Chelsea, New York — Dezeen 1 April 2019; Architectural Record May 2018; Wallpaper* 14 May 2018.** First Bjarke-Ingels-designed project under his WeWork chief-architect tenure; designed "to undo the compartmentalization often found in traditional school environments" (per Architect Magazine and Dezeen 1 April 2019). Cited as the named published example of WeWork's late-2010s integrated coworking-plus-amenity master-plan ambition; useful when the user wants the campus-scale variant rather than a single floor plate.
7. **Industry City / Cowork IC, Sunset Park, Brooklyn — industrycity.com Cowork IC brochure 2025; brooklyneagle.com 8 April 2025 ("Industry City surpasses 650 businesses").** 35-acre adaptive-reuse coworking campus inside the historic Bush Terminal manufacturing buildings; 6,000 sq ft Makerspace + photo studio, soundproof phone booths, three heated courtyards. Reference for adaptive-reuse coworking at *campus* (not floor) scale.

## Common mistakes

1. **Treating coworking as "office with a kitchenette."** The published genre is structurally hospitality + flagship — Bjarke Ingels' brief at WeWork was framed as "buildings, campuses, and neighborhoods" rather than as office floor plates (per Wallpaper* 14 May 2018; Architectural Record May 2018). Fix: budget at least 25-30% of floor area for non-desk amenities (lounge, conversation pit, library, plant volume, café).
2. **Open-plan as a parallel-bench grid.** None of the named projects use parallel benches. Second Home meanders, The Wing breaks the floor with archways and conversation pits (per Dezeen 14 February 2017; Dezeen 28 February 2018). Fix: replace at least one straight-line bench run with a wavy desk, a circular table, or a sofa-pit cluster.
3. **Decorative-only planting.** The published density is 1,000+ specimens functioning as architecture (per ArchDaily Second Home London project page; Dezeen 14 February 2017 Lisboa). Three potted ferns on a credenza is the *opposite* of biophilic coworking. Fix: ring every desk-perimeter and stair-edge with continuous plant cover, target 1 plant per 1m of desk-edge.
4. **Picking a brand color and using it only in the logo.** The Wing carries "Wing pink" through wall paint, poured concrete, granite tables, wallpaper, and archways (per Dezeen 28 February 2018; Dezeen 4 December 2017). Fix: when the project has a brand color, render it in *at least three* of (paint, soft furnishing, floor surface, fixture metal, wallpaper, signage).
5. **Cool-white office-grid ceiling lighting.** None of the named coworking interiors use this; the published mode is daylight + warm 2700-3000K LED + scattered vintage table lamps + brass sconces (per Dezeen 4 December 2017 The Wing SoHo; ArchDaily Second Home Lisboa specifications). Fix: design a top-light strategy first (skylight, clerestory, deep window), then layer lamps and sconces; if the ceiling has a fluorescent grid, the genre is wrong.
