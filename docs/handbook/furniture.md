# Furniture Types and Standard Dimensions

> Sources: Neufert *Architects' Data* (current ed., Wiley-Blackwell); Panero & Zelnik *Human Dimension & Interior Space* (Whitney Library of Design, 1979); GB/T 3324-2024 *General technical requirements for wooden furniture* (Standardization Administration of China, SAC/TC 480); ANSI/BIFMA X5.1-2017 (R2022) *General-Purpose Office Chairs — Tests* and BIFMA G1-2013 *Ergonomics Guideline for Furniture Used in Office Work Spaces*; UK National Bed Federation size chart; ISPA (International Sleep Products Association) U.S. mattress sizes.
>
> Last updated: 2026-04-29

## Purpose

This chapter is the typology + dimensions reference. When the picker pulls a sofa off Sketchfab or Polyhaven, two things must hold: (a) the asset is the *kind* of furniture the room needs (a 2-seater is not a sectional), and (b) its bounding-box dimensions sit inside the bands below. If the import is more than ~10% off the band, reject and search again — Sketchfab uploads are routinely mis-scaled.

Audience is twofold: the automated post-import checker that compares object dimensions against this table, and a non-designer user who needs to know whether "sofa" means 1.6 m, 2.1 m, or 2.7 m of wall.

## Sofas and seating

Soft-seating dimensions per Neufert "Living rooms / soft seating" chapter and Panero & Zelnik "Living Spaces / Sofa Seating" plate. Seat heights stay in a tight 0.40-0.46 m band because the floor of the band is set by popliteal height of the 5th-percentile female and the ceiling by foot-on-floor comfort for the 95th-percentile male — anthropometric rules, not style choices.

## Dining tables

Per Neufert "Restaurants and dining" chapter (4th English edition pp. 187-189 reproduced at https://www.scribd.com/document/932686691/Neufert-4th-edition-1-187-189): each diner needs ~600 mm of edge for a place setting and 300-400 mm of depth, table top sits at 0.75 m, dining-chair seat at 0.40-0.45 m. Centre-of-table service zone adds ~200 mm for shared dishes; if the meal is plated, an 800 × 800 mm square serves 4 with ~700 mm of working depth per cover. Round tables need ~50 mm more diameter than the equivalent rectangle's short axis to keep place-setting wedges usable.

## Beds

Two reference systems coexist. International (UK National Bed Federation, ISPA U.S., EU) names sizes; the Chinese consumer market names them by metric width. A 1.5 m Chinese bed equals a UK Double almost exactly; a 1.8 m bed equals a UK Super King. Frame outer dimensions add 50-150 mm to each side of the mattress for headboard, side rails, and slat overhang.

## Storage

Storage cabinetry is modular, and the dominant module across both kitchen and wardrobe markets is **600 mm**. Wardrobes are 600 mm deep because a clothes hanger across the shoulders is ~550 mm; kitchen base cabinets are 600 mm deep because that is the worktop overhang plus the appliance front-to-back depth (per Neufert *Architects' Data* "Kitchens" chapter, exact section not verified, cf. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Neufert; corroborated by industry standard summary at https://fittedbedroomwardrobes.com/blog/standard-kitchen-cabinet-sizes/).

The seat-to-surface offset is consistent: ~280-310 mm between seat top and worksurface top across all of the above except sofa (where the coffee-table top sits at or just below sofa-seat height, since a coffee table is not a working surface). Hold this offset when choosing a non-standard pairing.

## Worked examples

### Example 1 — pairing a 3-seat sofa with a coffee table (lounge layout)

A 3-seat Chesterfield at ~2100 × 950 × 440 (W × D × seat-H, mm) — see the Chesterfield row above. Coffee table:

- length: 0.55-0.66 × sofa width = 1155-1385 → choose 1200 mm
- depth: 600-700 mm (Panero & Zelnik audiovisual / occasional-table band, per https://www.scribd.com/document/361727715/PANERO-e-ZELNIK-Human-Dimensions-Interior-Spaces-pdf)
- top height: 380-460 mm (per Panero & Zelnik 1979) — choose 420 mm to sit just below the 440 mm sofa seat
- gap between sofa face and table: 400-460 mm (per `spatial.md` "Sofa-to-coffee-table")

So a 1200 × 650 × 420 mm coffee table, 430 mm in front of the sofa face. Total floor footprint of cluster: 2100 mm × (950 + 430 + 650 = 2030) mm.

### Example 2 — sizing a 6-cover dining setup in a 4 × 3 m alcove

6-person rectangular: choose 1800 × 1000 mm (top of the 6-person band). Chair pull-back per `spatial.md` is 760-915 mm each long side, so total occupied: 1000 + 760 + 760 = 2520 mm depth and 1800 + 760 (one end-server) = 2560 mm length, fits inside 4 × 3 m with ~440 mm circulation to one wall and ~480 mm to the other. Table top 750 mm, chairs at 450 mm seat per Neufert p. 187 (https://www.scribd.com/document/932686691/Neufert-4th-edition-1-187-189).

## Common mistakes

- **Sketchfab "sofa" imported at 0.6 m wide** — the asset was modelled as a doll-house prop or scaled to inches read as metres. A real 3-seat is 1.9-2.3 m. Rule: if `bound_box_x < 1.4 m`, reject as "not a sofa".
- **Chair seat at 600 mm** — that is a counter stool, not a dining chair. Dining chairs sit at 400-480 mm; if the picker brought back a 600 mm seat for a dining setup, the table will hit the diner mid-thigh.
- **Bar stool used at a 750 mm dining table** — the diner's knees crash into the apron. Stool seat must be ≤480 mm for a 750 mm table; for an 1050 mm bar, use a 740-810 mm stool.
- **"King bed" imported at 1500 mm wide** — the asset was modelled to UK King (1500 × 2000) but the room brief is US King (1930 × 2030) or Chinese 1.8 m. Always disambiguate by metric width before searching.
- **Wardrobe modelled at 450 mm depth** — hangers will not clear. Below 550 mm depth a wardrobe is a coat-hook strip, not a wardrobe; reject.
- **Kitchen base cabinet at 500 mm depth** — does not align to the 600 mm worktop standard; the worktop will overhang front and back unevenly. Snap to 560-600 mm.
