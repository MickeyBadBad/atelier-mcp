# Space Type: Retail Boutique (精品零售 / 专卖店)

UCEB civbook archive PDF); *Time-Saver Standards for Building Types* (3rd ed., Commercial section on Scribd); GB 50034-2013 《建筑照明设计标准》Table 5.2.3 商业建筑照明标准值 (gongbiaoku / zlglpt reproductions; Code of China English version); IBC 2021 §309 Mercantile Group M and Table 1004.5 Occupant Load (UpCodes Texas IBC 2021; ICC Digital Codes); ADA 2010 Standards for Accessible Design (accessibilitychecker.org summary); Designboom feature on i29 Architects' Frame Store Amsterdam (Frame magazine); FormRoom Frame magazine retail-trends piece. Last updated: 2026-04-29.

## Purpose

A retail boutique is a specialty shop where merchandise display drives the entire spatial logic — the customer journey, the lighting, the fixture grid, the cashwrap location, and the fitting-room cluster all serve one job: convert browsing into purchase while reinforcing brand identity. This chapter is the source of truth for AI agents scaffolding a small-format retail space (≈ 50–300 m² sales floor, 1–6 staff). Larger department stores, supermarkets, and shopping-mall anchor tenants are out of scope — those are typed separately. Downstream consumers are the `interior-discovery-intake` and `interior-plain-language-edit` skills plus the 9-dimension quality gates.

## Typical area range

| Region / context | Sales-floor range (m²) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Boutique (apparel, accessories, lifestyle), street-front | 50–150 m² sales floor | (per Neufert *Architects' Data* 4th ed. RETAIL chapter "specialised shops" typology; Time-Saver Standards 3rd ed. Commercial section "small shops" band of 5.3–6.0 m frontage × ~ 12–18 m depth) |
| Boutique flagship / experiential | 150–300 m² sales floor | (per Neufert 4th ed. RETAIL chapter "specialised shops / flagships" typology; FormRoom Frame magazine feature on the experiential-retail surge with larger brand-world flagships) |
| Mall / centre tenant slot | 7.3–9.2 m frontage, 12–18 m depth = 90–165 m² | (per Time-Saver Standards 3rd ed. Commercial: "large-space users at 7.3 to 9.2 m" frontage; small-shop band 5.3–6.0 m mostly 5.4 m) |

Density check: per IBC 2021 Table 1004.5, mercantile ground-floor occupant-load factor is **30 gross sq ft / person** (≈ 2.79 m²/person), upper floors **60 gross sq ft / person** (≈ 5.57 m²/person), and stockrooms **300 gross sq ft / person** (≈ 27.87 m²/person) (per IBC 2021 Table 1004.5 reproduced on UpCodes Texas IBC 2021 and Dalkita "Understanding Occupant Load" summary). A 100 m² ground-floor boutique therefore designs to ≈ 36 occupants — which sets the minimum aisle, exit, and accessible-route widths below.

Time-Saver Standards' loose-pack guidance is **4.6–7.0 m² per person** for "retail shops and showrooms" — a useful sanity check that lands between the IBC ground-floor and upper-floor factors (per Time-Saver Standards 3rd ed. Commercial: "retail shops and showrooms allow 4.6 to 7.0 m² per person").

## Fixture inventory

### Must-have

- **Cashwrap / point-of-sale counter** — anchors the transaction zone, sized to the standing 915–965 mm service height with an accessible 915 mm-high panel for wheelchair customers (per Time-Saver Standards Commercial counter-height; ADA 2010 §904.4.1).
- **Perimeter wall fixtures** (slatwall, rail, shelves) running the long walls — boutiques drive the highest density of merchandise visibility from perimeter elevations rather than mid-floor islands (per Neufert 4th ed. RETAIL "specialised shops" typology, perimeter-loaded plan).
- **Mid-floor fixture islands or tables** sized so that primary aisles around them stay ≥ 1500 mm and secondary aisles ≥ 900 mm (per Neufert 4th ed. RETAIL aisle widths; ADA 2010 §403.5 accessible 915 mm minimum).
- **Fitting room cluster** of 2–4 rooms for an apparel boutique, with at least one accessible room at 1525 × 1525 mm clear (per ADA 2010 §603; AccessibilityChecker ADA-retail summary).
- **Storefront window display** — the shop's first impression and the principal feature-lighting target (per FormRoom Frame magazine retail-trends piece on experiential storefronts; Designboom feature on i29's Frame Store Amsterdam where window installations carry the brand identity).

### Common

- Stockroom / back-of-house door directly behind or adjacent to the cashwrap, sized for one-person carry (≥ 900 mm clear) (per Neufert 4th ed. RETAIL service-flow diagrams; IBC 2021 §1010 egress-door minimums).
- Mannequin platforms or focal product plinths along the customer-flow spine (per Time-Saver Standards Commercial "varied plans" layout strategy: "the layout path can take any shape and create a design pattern").
- Mirror at the fitting-room exit corridor, full-length (≥ 1500 mm tall) (per Neufert 4th ed. RETAIL fitting-room diagrams).
- Small seating bench near the fitting-room cluster for companions / shoe try-on (per Time-Saver Standards Commercial customer-comfort guidance; suggested seat height 21–22 in / 530–560 mm).

### Optional

- Centre-floor service desk or styling station for high-touch boutiques (per Neufert 4th ed. RETAIL "specialised shops" typology when service ratio exceeds self-serve).
- Press-wall or brand-story moment near the entry — a non-merchandised wall reserved for editorial photography or installation (per Designboom feature on i29 Frame Store Amsterdam triangular display boards as a brand-narrative device).
- Iced-water / coffee bar for flagship-tier hospitality (per FormRoom Frame magazine experiential-retail trend coverage).

## Lighting layer recipe

| Layer | Target (lx) | Kelvin | Fixture | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ambient (general sales floor) | 300 lx maintained, 0.75 m horizontal plane, UGR ≤ 22, Ra ≥ 80 — general boutique | 2700–3500 K (warm-to-neutral, brand-dependent) | Recessed downlights or linear cove on a regular grid | (per GB 50034-2013 Table 5.2.3 row "一般商店营业厅": 300 lx, 0.75 m horizontal plane, UGR 22, Ra 80) |
| Ambient (premium boutique) | 500 lx maintained, 0.75 m horizontal plane, UGR ≤ 22, Ra ≥ 80 | 3000–3500 K (neutral-warm to flatter materials) | Recessed downlights, tighter spacing or higher lumen output | (per GB 50034-2013 Table 5.2.3 row "高档商店营业厅": 500 lx, 0.75 m horizontal plane, UGR 22, Ra 80) |
| Feature / accent on merchandise | 750–1500 lx on the merchandise face; ratio 3:1 to 5:1 vs ambient (theatrical effect at 1:15 not recommended for boutiques) | 3000 K, Ra ≥ 90 for colour-critical apparel | Adjustable track spots, framing projectors | (per GB 50034-2013 §6.3.4 — accent-lighting LPD allowance adds 5 W/m² over the base sales-floor budget — and SLL Code / IESNA retail guidance "1500 lux at the entrance and 2000 lux on the merchandise" cited in retail-lighting reference compilations) |
| Cashwrap task | 500 lx maintained on counter top, Ra ≥ 80 | 3000 K | Pendant or under-shelf linear over the counter | (per GB 50034-2013 Table 5.2.3 row "收款台": 500 lx on counter top, Ra 80) |
| Fitting-room task | 300–500 lx on a vertical plane at face height, Ra ≥ 90 | 3000 K, dual-side mirror lighting to remove under-eye shadows | Vertical sconces flanking the mirror, or perimeter cove | (per GB 50034-2013 Table 5.2.3 by analogy to changing-area / dressing-room rows; IES retail-lighting library guidance for high CRI in fitting environments) |

Operationalising the recipe:
- The **3:1 to 5:1 feature-to-ambient ratio** is the hard rule. Anything below 3:1 reads as flat warehouse lighting; anything above 10:1 produces dramatic theatre that punishes browsing comfort outside the spotlit zones (per IES retail-lighting library guidance summarised in the retail-lighting reference compilations cited above).
- **Colour rendering Ra ≥ 90** is required on apparel feature lights even though GB 50034-2013 Table 5.2.3 only mandates Ra ≥ 80 for the ambient layer — under-spec'd CRI hides red/blue shifts that customers discover at home and return (per IES retail-lighting library guidance; GB 50034-2013 §4.3 on colour rendering).
- **LPD allowance**: when accent lighting is in use, GB 50034-2013 §6.3.4 explicitly raises the lighting-power-density limit by 5 W/m² over the base sales-floor budget — design within this envelope (per GB 50034-2013 §6.3.4).
- Fitting-room CRI is non-negotiable: this is the moment the customer evaluates fit AND colour. Use Ra ≥ 90 sources at 3000 K with vertical illumination from both sides of the mirror (per IES retail-lighting library guidance).

## Worked examples

**100 m² street-front apparel boutique, ground floor, single entry.**
Sales floor 80 m² + stockroom 15 m² + fitting cluster 5 m². Per IBC 2021 Table 1004.5, occupant load = (80 / 2.79) + (15 / 27.87) + (5 / 2.79) ≈ 29 + 0.5 + 1.8 ≈ **31 occupants** (per IBC 2021 Table 1004.5, mercantile 30 gross sq ft / person ground floor; storage 300 gross sq ft / person). Below the 50-occupant threshold for panic hardware, single exit with 815 mm clear door is sufficient (per IBC 2021 §1010 + §1006). Two perimeter rail walls + two mid-floor tables + one cashwrap; primary spine 1700 mm wide, secondary 1000 mm wide, ADA-compliant aisle past every fixture (per Neufert 4th ed. RETAIL aisles; ADA 2010 §403.5). Ambient 300 lx at 3000 K (per GB 50034-2013 Table 5.2.3 一般商店); feature 1000 lx, Ra 90, on the front mannequin and the rear focal wall (per GB 50034-2013 §6.3.4 + IES retail-lighting library guidance).

**220 m² flagship lifestyle store, two-storey, mall tenant slot 8 m × 14 m.**
Ground floor 110 m² + upper floor 110 m². Occupant load = (110 / 2.79) + (110 / 5.57) ≈ 39 + 20 ≈ **59 occupants** (per IBC 2021 Table 1004.5). Above the 50-occupant threshold, panic hardware required on egress doors, and two exits required (per IBC 2021 §1006 / §1010). Ambient 500 lx, Ra 80 (per GB 50034-2013 Table 5.2.3 高档商店); feature 1500 lx, Ra 90 on hero merchandise zones; LPD allowance + 5 W/m² over the base budget per GB 50034-2013 §6.3.4. Two-storey ceiling height 3.6 m+ on ground per Time-Saver Standards Commercial.

## Common mistakes

- **Aisles narrower than 915 mm anywhere on the customer route.** Fails ADA 2010 §403.5 for accessible-route minimum and traps wheelchair customers (per ADA 2010 §403.5; AccessibilityChecker ADA-retail summary).
- **Cashwrap as a continuous wall with no accessible-height portion.** ADA 2010 §904.4.1 requires at least a 915 mm-wide section at ≤ 915 mm high — designing one single 1100 mm-high counter fails this (per ADA 2010 §904.4.1).
- **Feature-to-ambient ratio under 3:1.** Reads as flat fluorescent warehouse lighting and undercuts the boutique premium signal (per IES retail-lighting library guidance summarised in retail-lighting reference compilations).
- **Fitting-room mirror lit only from above.** Creates under-eye shadows that read as fatigue or illness; customers refuse to be photographed by themselves in such mirrors and the conversion drops. Use vertical sconces flanking the mirror with Ra ≥ 90 sources (per IES retail-lighting library guidance).
- **No window-display feature lighting.** GB 50034-2013 §6.3.4 explicitly funds the LPD for accent / feature lighting because the storefront is the boutique's first conversion surface — leaving the window dark fails the retail brief regardless of code compliance (per GB 50034-2013 §6.3.4; FormRoom / Designboom Frame Store Amsterdam storefront-as-brand principle).
- **Stockroom counted as sales area in occupant-load calculation.** IBC 2021 Table 1004.5 explicitly separates mercantile sales (30 gross sq ft / person) from storage (300 gross sq ft / person) — collapsing them inflates the apparent occupant load and forces unnecessary egress (per IBC 2021 Table 1004.5).
